## ०१

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

छ᳘न्दस्या उ᳘पदधात्य᳘त्रैष स᳘र्वोऽग्निः स᳘ꣳ᳘स्कृतः स᳘ एषो᳘ऽत्र श्रि᳘यमैच्छ᳘न्नो हैषो᳘ऽतः पुरा त᳘स्मा अ᳘लमास यच्छ्रि᳘यम᳘धारयिष्यत्त᳘स्मादिदमप्येत᳘र्ह्याहुर्न वा᳘ एषो᳘ऽलꣳ श्रियै धा᳘रणाय राज्या᳘य वा ग्रामणीछ्याय वे᳘ति त᳘स्मै देवा᳘ एताꣳ श्रि᳘यं प्रा᳘यच्छन्नेता᳘श्छन्द᳘स्याः पश᳘वो वै छ᳘न्दाꣳस्य᳘न्नं पशवो᳘ऽन्नमु श्रीः᳘ ॥१॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

He now lays down the chandasyā bricks. Now here this Agni-altar has become complete. He (the altar) now wished for Śrī (distinction). Because before this it was incomplete and was not qualified to own distinction or Śrī That is why it is said (in particular cases) that so and so person is not de- serving to have the distinction to assume the leadership of a country or village community. Gods gave these chandasyās bricks to confer Śrī (distinc- tion) to the altar. Chandasyās are same as cattle and chandasyās are food. Cattle and food constitute Śrī.
</details>


## ०२

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

त्रिचान्यु᳘पदधाति त्रि᳘वृद्वै᳘ पशुः᳘ पिता᳘ माता᳘ पुत्रो᳘ऽथो ग᳘र्भ उ᳘ल्बं जराय्व᳘थो त्रिवृद्वा अ᳘न्नं कृषिर्वृ᳘ष्टिर्बी᳘जमे᳘कैवा᳘तिछन्दा भवत्ये᳘का ह्येव सा स᳘र्वाणि छन्दाꣳस्य᳘ति सा या सा श्री᳘र्महत्त᳘दुक्थं तद्यत्त᳘न्मह᳘दुक्थ᳘मेतास्ता᳘श्छन्द᳘स्याः ॥२॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

He lays them in threes. Cattle is threefold; namely, the father, the mother and the son. Or namely, embryo, placenta and chorion (jarāyu). Food also is threefold; namely cultivation, rain and seed. Out of these, one is an excessive meter (Atichandas). For even though it is one, it is bigger than the other metres. That distinction (of the atichandas) lies in the great hymn of praise (ukthya) and as to that great hymn of praise are those chandasyās (bricks of metres).
</details>


## ०३

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

त᳘स्य शि᳘रो गाय᳘त्र्य आत्मा᳘ त्रिष्टुभो᳘ऽनूकं ज᳘गत्यः पक्षौ᳘ पङ्क्तयो᳘ऽथैता᳘सां ककु᳘भां चत्वा᳘रिचत्वार्यक्ष᳘राण्यादाया᳘तिच्छन्दस्यु᳘पदधाति सा᳘ सा᳘तिच्छन्दा एव भवति गाय᳘त्र्य इतराः सं᳘पद्यन्ते᳘ सैव᳘ गायत्र्यशीतिर्या᳘ बृहत्यः᳘ सा बा᳘र्हती या᳘ उष्णि᳘हः सो᳘ष्णिह्य᳘थ यद्वि᳘शो य᳘दर्धर्चौ य᳘दैन्द्राग्नं य᳘दावप᳘नं तद᳘तिच्छन्दा अ᳘थ य᳘न्नदो यत्सू᳘ददोहा य᳘त्पदनु᳘षङ्गा यत्किञ्चा᳘त्रानुष्टुप्कर्मी᳘णं त᳘दनुष्टु᳘भः ॥३॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

Gāyatrī verses are its head; Triṣṭubh verses its body; Jagatī verses its spine and the Paṅktī verses are its wings; out of each of the Kakubh verses, he takes four syllables and adds them to the atichandas. That is how it be- comes one with atichandas. The others become Gāyatrīs. This is that eighty count Gāyatrī. From the Bṛhatīs, the Bārhatā is made up and from Uṣṇiks, the Auṣṇiha. The half-Ṛks are the Vasu, the Aindrāgnī and insertion — they are Atichandas. As to the Nāda verse, the Sūdadohā, the pāda appendages and whatever Anuṣṭhubh there is – they constitute the atichandas.
</details>


## ०४

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

प्रतिष्ठा द्वि᳘पदा एता᳘वद्वै᳘ मह᳘दुक्थं᳘ मह᳘दुक्थꣳ श्रीः स᳘र्वामे᳘वास्मा एतां᳘ देवाः श्रि᳘यं प्रा᳘यच्छꣳस्त᳘थै᳘वास्मा अय᳘मेताꣳ स᳘र्वाꣳ श्रि᳘यं प्र᳘यच्छति ॥४॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

The Dvipadā verses form the foot-hold. This much constitute the Mahaduktha (great hymn of praise) and this great hymn of praise is what is known as Śrī (distinction). The gods conferred on him (Agni-altar) all that Śrī. In the same manner, this Yajamāna confers on him all that distinction.
</details>


## ०५

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

य᳘द्वेव᳘ छन्द᳘स्या उपद᳘धात्येतद्वै᳘ देवा᳘ एतं नाक᳘ꣳ स्वर्गं लोक᳘मपश्यन्नेता स्तो᳘मभागास्तस्मिन्नविꣳशस्ते᳘षां विशतां᳘ प्रजा᳘पतिरुत्त᳘मोऽविशत्स यः स᳘ प्रजा᳘पतिरेतास्ताश्छन्दस्याः ॥५॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

Why he places the chandasyā bricks is also this — gods saw the heaven in the form of stomabhāgas, that is the nāka and they entered it. While they were entering; Prajāpati entered last. That Prajāpati is these chandasyās.
</details>


## ०६

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

त᳘स्य शि᳘रो गाय᳘त्र्यस्ता य᳘द्गायत्र्यो भ᳘वन्ति गायत्रꣳ हि शि᳘रस्तिस्रो᳘ भवन्ति त्रिवृद्धि शि᳘रः पूर्वार्ध उ᳘पदधाति पुरस्ता᳘द्धीदꣳ शिरः ॥६॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

His head is Gāyatrī. As to its being Gāyatrī, it is because the head is of the nature of Gāyatrī. They are three for the head is threefold. They are placed in the front side because this head is in the front.
</details>


## ०७

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

उ᳘रस्त्रिष्टु᳘भस्ता᳘ रेतः सि᳘चोर्वेलायो᳘पदधाति पृष्ट᳘यो वै᳘ रेतः सि᳘चा उ᳘रो वै प्र᳘तिपृ᳘ष्टयः ॥७॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

The Triṣṭubhs constitue the chest. They are placed between the retassica bricks because retassicas are the side bones. The side bones lie against the chest.
</details>


## ०८

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

श्रो᳘णी ज᳘गत्यः स या᳘वति पुर᳘स्तात्स्वयमातृण्णा᳘यै त्रिष्टु᳘भ उपद᳘धाति ता᳘वति पश्चाज्ज᳘गतीर्यो वा᳘ अयं म᳘ध्ये प्राणस्त᳘देषा᳘ स्वयमातृण्णा या᳘वत्यु वा᳘ एत᳘स्मात्प्राणा᳘त्पुर᳘स्तादु᳘रस्ता᳘वति पश्चाच्छ्रो᳘णी ॥८॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

The Jagatīs constitute the hips. These are placed so much behind the naturally perforated bricks as the triṣṭubh bricks are placed before the naturally perforated ones. Because the naturally perforated bricks are the vital airs in the middle. As far from that vital air as the chest is in front, so far are the hips behind the vital air.
</details>


## ०९

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

स᳘क्थ्यावनुष्टु᳘भस्ता अ᳘नन्तर्हिता ज᳘गतीभ्य उ᳘पदधात्य᳘नन्तर्हिते तच्छ्रो᳘णिभ्याꣳ स᳘क्थ्यौ दधाति ॥९॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

The Anuṣṭubhs are the thighs. They should be placed close to the jagatī bricks. Thereby the thighs are placed close to the hips.
</details>


## १०

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

प᳘र्शवो बृह᳘त्यः की᳘कसा ककु᳘भः सो᳘ऽन्तरेण त्रिष्टु᳘भश्च ककु᳘भश्च बृहतीरुपदधाति त᳘स्मादिमा उभय᳘त्र प᳘र्शवो बद्धाः की᳘कसासु च जत्रुषु च ॥१०॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

The Bṛhatīs are the ribs and Kakubhs are the bones of the breast. He places the Bṛhatīs between the Triṣṭubhs and Kakubhs, whence these bones got fashioned on either side by the breast-bone and the ribs.
</details>


## ११

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

ग्रीवा᳘ उष्णिहस्ता अ᳘नन्तर्हिता गायत्री᳘भ्य उ᳘पदधात्य᳘नन्तर्हितास्त᳘च्छीर्ष्णो᳘ ग्रीवा᳘ दधाति ॥११॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

The Uṣṇiks are the neck. He places them close to the Gāyatrīs and thereby he places the neck near the head.
</details>


## १२

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

प᳘क्षौ पङ्क्त्य᳘स्ता य᳘त्पङ्क्त्यो᳘ भ᳘वन्ति पा᳘ङ्क्तौ हि᳘ पक्षौ᳘ पार्श्वत उ᳘पदधाति पार्श्वतो᳘ हीमौ᳘ पक्षौ यद्व᳘र्षीयश्च्छ᳘न्दस्त᳘छक्षिणत उ᳘पदधाति द᳘क्षिणं तद᳘र्धं पशो᳘र्वी᳘र्यवत्तरं करोति तस्माद्दक्षिणो᳘ऽर्धः पशो᳘र्वी᳘र्यवत्तरः ॥१२॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

The Paṅktīs are the wings. Why they are Paṅktīs is because the wings are Paṅkti or of fivefold nature. He places them on the sides because the wings are on the sides. That metre which is larger is placed on the right side and thus makes the right side of the animal stronger. That is why the right half of animal's body is stronger.
</details>


## १३

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

उद᳘रम᳘तिच्छन्दाः पश᳘वो वै छ᳘न्दाꣳस्य᳘न्नं पश᳘व उद᳘रमु वा अ᳘न्नमत्युद᳘रꣳ हि वा अ᳘न्नम᳘त्ति त᳘स्माद्य᳘दोद᳘रम᳘न्नं प्राप्नोत्य᳘थ त᳘ज्जग्धं यात᳘यामरूपं भवति तद्य᳘देषा᳘ पशूꣳश्छ᳘न्दाꣳस्य᳘त्ति त᳘स्माद᳘तिच्छन्दा अ᳘त्ति छन्दा ह वै ताम᳘तिच्छन्दा इत्या᳘चक्षते परो᳘ऽक्षं परो᳘क्षकामा᳘ हि देवाः ॥१३॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

The atichandas constitute the belly. Because, the metres are cattle and cattle are food. It is the belly that consumes the food. Therefore when the food reaches the belly, it becomes eaten and assumes the digested form. In as much as these (atichandas bricks) eat (atti) the metres, namely cattle, they are called atichandas. Atichandas is what is mystically called atichandas. For, gods preferred concealed names.
</details>


## १४

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

यो᳘निः पु᳘रीषवती ते स᳘ꣳ᳘स्पृष्टे उ᳘पदधाति स᳘ꣳ᳘स्पृष्टे᳘ ह्युद᳘रं च यो᳘निश्च पु᳘रीषसꣳहिते भवतो माꣳसं वै पु᳘रीषं माꣳसे᳘न वा᳘ उद᳘रं च यो᳘निश्च स᳘ꣳ᳘हिते पूर्वा᳘तिच्छन्दा भ᳘वत्य᳘परा पुरीषवत्यु᳘त्तर᳘ꣳ᳘ ह्युद᳘रमधरा यो᳘निः ॥१४॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

A brick (spread over ) with loose soil (purīṣavatī), constitutes the womb. These two (atichandas and the purīṣavatī) are laid down close to each other. Because, the belly and the womb are close to each other. Both these are covered by loose soil. Purīṣa or loose soil is flesh. The belly and the womb are connected with flesh. The front one is an atichanda and behind is the purīṣavatī; because the belly is above and the womb is below.
</details>


## १५

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

ते प्रा᳘च्या उ᳘पदधाति प्रा᳘ङ् ह्ये᳘षोऽग्नि᳘श्चीयते᳘ऽथो प्रा᳘ग्वै प्रा᳘च उद᳘रं प्रा᳘ची यो᳘निर्बहिस्तोमभागꣳ हृ᳘दयं वै स्तो᳘मभागा हृ᳘दयमु वा᳘ उत्तमम᳘थोद᳘रम᳘थ यो᳘निः ॥१५॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

He places them projecting eastwards; because this Agni-altar is built up (tending) towards the east. When one moves forward, both his belly and womb move forward. These are placed outside the stomabhāgas, for the stomabhāgas are the heart. Because the head is highest, then come the belly and the womb.
</details>


## १६

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

ते᳘ दक्षि᳘णतः᳘ स्वयमातृण्णा᳘या उ᳘पदधात्य᳘थ प्रथमा᳘यां चि᳘ता उत्तरतः᳘ स्वयमातृण्णा᳘या उद᳘रं च यो᳘निं चो᳘पदधाति यो वा᳘ अयं म᳘ध्ये प्राणस्त᳘देषा᳘ स्वयमातृ᳘ण्णैत᳘स्य त᳘त्प्राण᳘स्योभय᳘त उद᳘रं च यो᳘निं च दधाति त᳘स्मादेत᳘स्य प्राण᳘स्योभय᳘त उद᳘रं च यो᳘निश्च ॥१६॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

He places them to the right of the naturally perforated brick. Now in the first layer, he placed both the belly and the womb to the north of the Svayamātṛṇṇā. Because that naturally perforated one is indeed the vital air in the middle. Thereby he places the belly and the womb on both sides of that centrally situated vital air. That is why the belly and the womb are on either sides of the central vital air.
</details>


## १७

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

प्रतिष्ठा द्वि᳘पदास्ता यद्द्विपदा भ᳘वन्ति द्वन्द्वꣳ हि᳘ प्रतिष्ठा᳘ तिस्रो᳘ भवन्ति त्रिवृद्धि᳘ प्रतिष्ठा᳘ पश्चादुपदधाति पश्चा᳘द्धीयं प्रतिष्ठा ॥१७॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

The Dvipadās are the feet. Why they are Dvipadā is because a pair confers stability. There are three such verses; for a stand is threefold (with two feet and a tail). He lays them down at the back, for the feet are at the back (bottom) of the body.
</details>


## १८

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

सोऽस्त्येष सु᳘कृत᳘ आत्मा तद्य᳘स्य हैतमेवꣳ सु᳘कृतमात्मानं कुर्वन्त्येत᳘ꣳ ह स सु᳘कृतमात्मा᳘नमभिसं᳘भवत्य᳘थ य᳘स्य हैतम᳘तोऽन्य᳘था कुर्व᳘न्ति दु᳘ष्कृतꣳ ह त᳘स्यात्मा᳘नं कुर्वन्ति स᳘ ह दु᳘ष्कृतमे᳘वात्मा᳘नमभिसं᳘भवति ॥१८॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

Thus that body of his (Agni-altar) is well made. He whose body is so well made, his body becomes complete. He whose body is not thus well made, his body remains incomplete.
</details>


## १९

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

त᳘देते᳘ सामनिधने᳘ऽभ्युक्ते अर्कौ᳘ देवा᳘नां परमे व्योमन्नर्क᳘स्य देवाः᳘ परमे᳘ व्योमन्नि᳘त्येतद्वै᳘ देवा᳘नां विशतां᳘ प्रजा᳘पतिरुत्त᳘मोऽविशत्त᳘स्मादाहार्को᳘ देवा᳘नां परमे᳘ व्योमन्नित्य᳘थ यदा᳘हार्क᳘स्य देवाः᳘ परमे᳘ व्योमन्नि᳘त्ययं᳘ वा᳘ अग्नि᳘रर्कस्त᳘स्यैतदुत्तमायां चि᳘तौ स᳘र्वे देवा᳘ विष्टास्त᳘स्मादाहार्क᳘स्य देवाः᳘ परमे᳘ व्यो᳘मन्नि᳘ति ॥१९॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

It is keeping in mind this fact that the two Nidhana Sāmans (Sāma hymns marking the completion) are chanted —“The light in the highest realm of the gods” and “the gods are in highest heaven of the light.” At that time, when gods were entering (the nāka), Prajāpati was the last to enter. That is why he says, “The light is in the highest realm of gods.” As to why he says, “The gods are in highest heaven of the light” is because, the light, doubtless, is the Agni-altar and it is on its highest layer where all the gods have thus entered. That is why he says “The gods are in the highest heaven of light.” (Second Brāhmaṇa Ends.)
</details>
