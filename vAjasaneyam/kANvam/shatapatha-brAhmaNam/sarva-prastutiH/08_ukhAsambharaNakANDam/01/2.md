## ०१

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

सो᳘ऽकामयत प्रजा᳘पतिर्भू᳘य एव᳘ स्यात्प्र᳘जायेते᳘ति सोऽग्नि᳘ना पृथिवीं᳘ मिथुनꣳ स᳘मभवत्त᳘त आ᳘ण्डꣳ स᳘मवर्त्त᳘त त᳘द᳘भ्यमृशत्पु᳘ष्यत्वि᳘ति पु᳘ष्यतु भू᳘योऽस्त्वि᳘त्येव त᳘दब्रवीत्स यो ग᳘र्भोऽन्तरा᳘सीत्स᳘ वायु᳘रसृज्यता᳘थ यद᳘श्रु सं᳘क्षरितमा᳘सीत्ता᳘नि वयाꣳस्यभवन्न᳘थ यः᳘ कपाले रसो लिप्त आ᳘सीत्ता मरीच᳘योऽभवन्न᳘थ य᳘त्कपालमा᳘सीत्तदन्त᳘रिक्षमभवत् ॥ १ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

That Prajāpati desired "May it (the earth) multiply again," He caused a pair by uniting with the earth in his form as Agni. There came into being an egg. He caressed it saying "May you grow! May you further multiply". And the embryo which was inside was created as vāyu (the wind). The tears that flowed became the birds; the juice that was adhering to the shell (of the egg) became those Sun-motes and that which was the shell became the atmosphere.
</details>


## ०२

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

सो᳘ऽकामयत भू᳘य एव᳘ स्यात्प्र᳘जायेते᳘ति स᳘ वायु᳘नान्त᳘रिक्षं मिथुनꣳ स᳘मभवत्त᳘त आण्डꣳ स᳘मवर्त्त᳘त त᳘द᳘भ्यमृशद्य᳘शो बिभृही᳘ति त᳘तोऽसा᳘वादि᳘त्योऽसृज्य᳘तैष वै यशो᳘ऽथ यद᳘श्रु सं᳘क्षरितमा᳘सीत्सोऽश्मा पृ᳘श्निरभवद᳘श्रुर्ह वै तम᳘श्मेत्या᳘चक्षते परो᳘क्षं परो᳘क्षकामा हि᳘ देवा अ᳘थ यः᳘ कपा᳘ले र᳘सो लिप्त आ᳘सीत्ते᳘ रश्मयोऽभवन्न᳘थ य᳘त्कपा᳘लमा᳘सीत्सा द्यौ᳘रभवत् ॥ २ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

He desired "May it multiply and reproduce". He caused a pair by uniting with the atmosphere in his form as vāyu. Thence, an egg appeared. He caressed it saying "Bear thou glory". From that, yonder Sun was produced. He is indeed glory. The tears that flowed became the multi-coloured pebbles (aśma). It is tear (aśru) itself which is called aśman (pebbles) in esoteric terms, since gods like concealing (in mystic appellations). Then the juice that was adhering to the egg's shell became those rays and that which was the shell became the sky.
</details>


## ०३

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

सो᳘ऽकामयत भू᳘य एव᳘ स्यात्प्र᳘जायेते᳘ति स᳘ आदित्ये᳘न दि᳘वं मिथुनꣳ स᳘मभवत्त᳘त आण्डꣳ स᳘मवर्त्त᳘त त᳘द᳘भ्यमृशद्रे᳘तो बिभृही᳘ति तत᳘श्चन्द्र᳘मा असृज्य᳘तैष वै रेतो᳘ऽथ यद᳘श्रु संक्षरितमा᳘सीत्ता᳘नि न᳘क्षत्राण्यभवन्नथ यः᳘ कपा᳘ले र᳘सो लिप्त आ᳘सीत्ता अवान्तरदि᳘शोऽभवन्न᳘थ य᳘त्कपालमा᳘सीत्ता दि᳘शोऽभवन् ॥ ३ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

He desired "May it multiply and reproduce". He caused a pair by uniting with the sky in his form as the Sun. Thence, came into being an egg. He caressed it saying "Bear thou seed". From that was created the Moon. Moon is indeed the seed. The tears that flowed became those stars. And the juice that was adhering to the egg's shell became those intermediate quarters and that which was the shell became the quarters (diśas).
</details>


## ०४

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

स᳘ इमाँल्लोका᳘न्त्सृष्ट्वा᳘कामयत ताः᳘ प्रजाः᳘ सृजेय या᳘ म एषु᳘ लोके᳘षु स्युरि᳘ति स म᳘नसा वा᳘चं मिथुनꣳ स᳘मभवत्सोऽष्टौ द्रप्सान्ग᳘र्भ्यभव᳘त्तेऽष्टौ वस᳘वोऽसृज्य᳘न्त तानस्यां उ᳘पादधात्स मनसैव वा᳘चं मिथु᳘नꣳ स᳘मभवत्स ए᳘काद᳘श द्रप्साङ्ग᳘र्ब्भ्यभवत ए᳘काद᳘शरुद्रा᳘ असृज्य᳘न्त ता᳘नन्त᳘रिक्ष उ᳘पादधात्स म᳘नसैव वा᳘चं मिथुनꣳ स᳘मभवत्स द्वा᳘दश द्रप्साङ्ग᳘र्भ्यभवत्ते द्वा᳘दशादित्या᳘ असृज्य᳘न्त ता᳘न्दिव्यु᳘पादधात्स म᳘नसैव वा᳘चं मिथुनꣳ स᳘मभवत्स᳘ ग᳘र्भ्यभवत्स᳘ वि᳘श्वान्देवा᳘नसृजत ता᳘न्दिक्षूपादधात् ॥ ४ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

Having created these worlds, he desired "May I create such progeny as shall be mine in these worlds". He caused a pair by uniting his speech (vāk) with his mind. He, with eight drops, became pregnant. Those (eight drops) produced eight Vasus; he stationed them on this earth. He caused a pair again by uniting his speech (vāk) with his mind. He, with eleven drops became pregnant. Those (eleven drops) got produced as eleven Rudras. He placed them in the atmosphere. He caused a pair again by uniting his speech (vāk) with his mind. He, with twelve drops, became pregnant. Those (twelve drops) got created as the twelve Ādityas. He stationed them in the sky. He caused a pair once more by uniting his speech (vāk) with his mind. He became pregnant and produced the Viśvedevas (all-gods). He settled them in the quarters.
</details>


## ०५

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अ᳘थो आहुः अग्नि᳘मेव᳘ सृष्टं व᳘सवो᳘ऽन्वसृज्य᳘न्त ता᳘नस्यामु᳘पादधाद्वा᳘युꣳ रुद्रास्ता᳘नन्त᳘रिक्ष आदित्य᳘मादित्यास्ता᳘न्दिवि वि᳘श्वेदेवा᳘श्चन्द्र᳘मसं तान्दिक्षू᳘पादधादि᳘ति ॥ ५ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

Hence they say Agni was created and then followed the creation of Vasus, whom he placed on the earth. After the creation of Vāyu, the Rudras came, whom he stationed in the atmosphere. After the creation of the Sun, the Ādityas came into being and were placed in the sky. Following the creation of the moon, the Viśvedevas (all-gods) were created, who were stationed in the quarters (diśas).
</details>


## ०६

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अ᳘थो आहुः प्रजा᳘पतिरेवेमाँल्लोका᳘न्त्सृष्ट्वा᳘ पृथिव्यां प्र᳘त्यतिष्ठत्त᳘स्मा इमा ओ᳘षधयो᳘ऽन्नमपच्यन्त त᳘दाश्नात्स᳘ ग᳘र्भ्यभवत्स᳘ ऊर्ध्वे᳘भ्य एव᳘ प्राणे᳘भ्यो देवान᳘सृजत येऽर्वाञ्चः प्राणास्ते᳘भ्यो म᳘र्त्याः प्रजा᳘ इत्य᳘तो यत्तमथा᳘सृजत तथासृजत प्रजा᳘पति᳘स्त्वे᳘वेदꣳ स᳘र्वमसृजत य᳘दिदं कि᳘ञ्च ॥ ६ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

So they say, Prajāpati himself having created these worlds got firmly established on the earth. From him, these plants matured to bear fruits (food) which he ate. He became pregnant. From the upper vital airs, he created gods and from those vital airs which were lower, the mortal beings. He thus created whatever has come to life. Prajāpati himself, therefore, created all these and whatsoever that came to exist.
</details>


## ०७

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

स᳘ प्रजाः᳘ सृष्ट्वा᳘ स᳘र्वमाजि᳘मित्वा᳘ व्यस्रꣳसत त᳘स्मादु हैतद्यः स᳘र्वमानिमे᳘ति᳘ व्येव᳘स्रꣳसते त᳘स्माद्वि᳘स्रस्तात्प्राणो᳘ मध्यत उ᳘दक्रामत्त᳘स्मिन्नेनमु᳘त्क्रान्ते देवा᳘ अजहुः ॥ ७ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

He having created the beings and having run the full race, became exhausted. That is why one who exerts to the full extent gets exhausted. From him, who was tired, the vital air departed from inside. When that went out, the gods abandoned (him).
</details>


## ०८

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

सोऽग्नि᳘मब्रवीत्त्वं᳘ मा सं᳘धेही᳘ति किं᳘ मे त᳘तो भविष्यती᳘ति त्व᳘या मा᳘चक्षान्तै यौ वै᳘ पुत्रा᳘णाꣳ रा᳘ध्यते ते᳘न पित᳘रं पितामहं᳘ पुत्रं पौ᳘त्रमा᳘चक्षते त्व᳘या मा᳘चक्षान्ता अ᳘थ मा सं᳘धेही᳘ति तथे᳘ति त᳘मग्निः स᳘मदधात्त᳘स्मादेतं᳘ प्रजा᳘पतिꣳ स᳘न्तमग्निम᳘ग्निरित्या᳘चक्षत आ᳘ह वा᳘ एनेन पित᳘रं पितामहं᳘ पुत्रं पौ᳘त्रं चक्षते य᳘ एवं वेद ॥ ८ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

He told Agni, you put me together (revive me). "What will I gain" he asked. Prajāpati said, "They shall call me along with you. For, which one of the sons succeeds, by him they refer to his father and to his grand-father (and also refer to him) as the son (of so and so) and the grand son (of so and so). So let me be called after you; hence revive me". Agni said "So it be" and revived him. Therefore (since thus revived by Agni) being Prajāpati, he when revived, is called Agni. Whosoever knows this, after him they call his father and grandfather (him) as son and grandfather.
</details>


## ०९

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

त᳘मब्रवीत्क᳘स्मिꣳस्त्वो᳘ पधास्यामी᳘ति हित᳘ एवे᳘त्यब्रवीत्प्रा᳘णो वै᳘ हितं᳘ प्राणो हि स᳘र्वेभ्यो भूते᳘भ्यो हितस्तद्य᳘देनꣳ हित᳘ उ᳘पादधात्त᳘स्मादाहो᳘पधास्याम्यु᳘पदधाम्यु᳘पादधामि᳘ति ॥ ९ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

Agni asked him, "Where shall I settle you?" "Settle me on the hita (a suitable good location)" he replied. The vital air is, indeed, hita (suitable) because the vital air is good for all beings. Since Prajāpati was set up on hita, (even now while setting up the Cityāgni) one says, "I shall set up; I am setting up and I have set up" (in future, present and past tenses).
</details>


## १०

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

त᳘दाहुः कि᳘ꣳ᳘ हितं किमु᳘पहितमि᳘ति प्राण᳘ एव᳘ हितं वा᳘गुपहितं प्राणे᳘ हीयं वागु᳘पेव हिता᳘ प्राण᳘स्त्वेव᳘ हितम᳘ङ्गान्यु᳘पहितं प्राणे᳘ ही᳘मान्य᳘ङ्गान्यु᳘पेव हिता᳘नि ॥ १० ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

They ask: "What is hita and what is upahita?" The vital air is the hita and speech is upahita; for, it is indeed on the vital air, this speech depends (upahita). The vital air is of course, the hita and the limbs are all upahita, because the limbs are dependent on the prāṇa.
</details>


## ११

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

सोऽस्यैष चि᳘त्य आसीच्चे᳘तव्यो᳘ ह्यस्या᳘सीत्त᳘स्माच्चि᳘त्यश्चि᳘त्य उ ए᳘वायं य᳘जमानस्य भवति चेत᳘व्यो᳘ ह्यस्य भ᳘वति त᳘स्माद्वे᳘व चित्यः ॥ ११ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

That Prajāpati was the citya (one to be revived) by Agni. Because he was to be revived, he became (known as) citya: for the sacrificer (Yajamāna) too, he happens.to be citya (one to be revived on the brick altar).
</details>


## १२

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

त᳘देता᳘ वा अस्य ताः प᳘ञ्च त᳘न्वो᳘ व्यस्र᳘ꣳ᳘सन्त लो᳘म त्व᳘ङ्मा᳘ꣳ᳘सम᳘स्थि म᳘ज्जा ता᳘ ए᳘वैताः प᳘ञ्च चितयस्तद्यत्प᳘ञ्च चि᳘तीश्चिनो᳘त्येता᳘भिरे᳘वैनं तत्त᳘नूभिश्चि᳘नोति य᳘च्चिनोति तस्माच्चित᳘यः ॥ १२ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

Now those were the five parts of Prajāpati's body that were weakened (when he got exhausted; viz. hair, skin, flesh, bone and marrow. These form the five layers (of the five altars) and by building up the five layers, he revives him by those body-parts. In as much as they are built up (a) they are citis (fire altars).
</details>


## १३

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

स᳘ यः स᳘ प्रजा᳘पतिर्व्यस्र᳘ꣳ᳘सत संवत्सरः सोऽथ या᳘ अस्य ताः प᳘ञ्च त᳘न्वो व्यस्र᳘ꣳ᳘सन्तर्त्ते᳘वस्ते प᳘ञ्च वा᳘ ऋत᳘वः प᳘ञ्चैताश्चि᳘तयस्त᳘द्यत्प᳘ञ्चचि᳘तीश्चिनो᳘त्यृतु᳘भिरे᳘वैनं त᳘च्चिनोति य᳘च्चिनोति त᳘स्माच्चि᳘तयः ॥ १३ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

That Prajāpati who got exhausted is the year and those five parts of his body which were weakened are the seasons; for, seasons are five and these layers (of the altar) are five. When five layers are being built up (for the altars), he is being revived verily by the seasons. In as much as they are built up, they are citis (fire altars).
</details>


## १४

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

स᳘ यः स᳘ संवत्सरः᳘ प्रजा᳘पतिर्व्यस्र᳘ꣳ᳘सताय᳘मेव स᳘ वायु᳘र्योऽयं प᳘वते᳘ऽथ या᳘ अस्य ता᳘ ऋतवः प᳘ञ्च त᳘न्वो व्य᳘स्रꣳसन्त दि᳘शस्ताः प᳘ञ्च वै दि᳘शः प᳘ञ्चैताश्चितयस्तद्यत्पञ्चचि᳘तीश्चि᳘नोति दिग्भि᳘रेवैनं त᳘च्चिनोति य᳘च्चिनोति त᳘स्माच्चितयोऽथ य᳘श्चि᳘तेऽग्नि᳘र्निधीय᳘तेऽसौ स᳘ आदित्यः स᳘ एष᳘ एवै᳘षोऽग्नि᳘श्चित एता᳘वन्नु तद्य᳘देनमग्निः᳘ समदधात् ॥ १४ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

That Prajāpati, the year, who became exhausted is that very vāyu (wind) which blows and those five parts of his body which got weakened, they are the quarters. Of course, the quarters are five and the citis (layers of the altar) are five. Therefore, when he builds up the five layers (of the altar) he actually builds them up with the quarters. In as much as they are being built up (a) they are citis (fire altars). And the Fire that is installed on that built up altar is that Yonder Sun. That same Agni is (now) set up. In this manner (process) Agni revived him.
</details>


## १५

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अ᳘थो आहुः प्रजा᳘पतिरे᳘व वि᳘स्रस्तो देवा᳘नब्रवीत्सं᳘ मा धत्ते᳘ ते᳘ देवा᳘ अग्निमब्रुवꣳस्त्व᳘यीमं᳘ पित᳘रं प्रजा᳘पतिं भिष᳘ज्यामे᳘ति स वा᳘ अह᳘मेत᳘स्मिन्स᳘र्वस्मिन्नेव᳘ विशानी᳘ति तथे᳘ति त᳘स्मादेतं प्रजा᳘पतिꣳ स᳘न्तमग्निरित्या᳘चक्षते ॥ १५ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

They say—Prajāpati, when exhausted, said to the Gods "Revive me". The gods told Agni—"In thee (with your help) we will cure this Prajāpati, our father". He (Agni) replied—"Then, I shall enter into him as a whole". They said "Yes". Hence, while being Prajāpati, they yet call him Agni.
</details>


## १६

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

तं᳘ देवा᳘ अग्नावा᳘हुतिभिर᳘भिषज्य᳘ꣳ᳘स्ते यां᳘ यामाहुतिम᳘जुहवुः सा᳘ सैनं पक्वे᳘ष्टका भूत्वा᳘प्यपद्य᳘त तद्य᳘दिष्टा᳘त्समभ᳘वꣳस्त᳘स्मादि᳘ष्टकास्त᳘स्मादग्निने᳘ष्टकाः पचन्त्या᳘हुतीरे᳘वैनास्त᳘त्कुर्वन्ति᳘ ॥ १६ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

They treated him by offering oblations into the fire. Whatever oblations were offered, they became baked bricks and entered into him. Because they were produced from the oblations (iṣṭa), they are called iṣṭakas (bricks). That is why they bake the bricks with fire. By so doing they are actually offering oblations.
</details>


## १७

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

सोऽब्रवीद्या᳘वद्यावद्वै᳘ जुहुथ ता᳘वत्ता᳘वन्मे कं᳘ भवती᳘ति तद्य᳘दस्मा इष्टे कम᳘भवत्त᳘स्माद्वे᳘वेष्टकाः ॥ १७ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

He said "As much as you offer oblations, so much I got 'kam'; happiness (cured)". As happiness or cure was due to the offering (iṣṭa), therefore also, they are iṣṭakas (bricks).
</details>


## १८

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

तद्ध स्माहा᳘क्ताक्ष्यो य᳘ एव य᳘जुष्मतीं भू᳘यसीरि᳘ष्टका विद्या᳘त्सोऽग्निं चिनुयाद्भू᳘य एव त᳘त्पित᳘रं प्रजा᳘पतिं भिषज्यती᳘ति ॥ १८ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

That is why, Aktākṣi said "Only he who knows numerous iṣṭakas (bricks) endowed with the Yajus mantras (formulas) should build up the fire-altar. Abun- dantly indeed, he cures father Prajāpati".
</details>


## १९

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अ᳘थ ह स्माह ता᳘ण्ड्यः क्षत्रं वै य᳘जुष्मत्य इ᳘ष्टका वि᳘शो लोकंपृणा᳘ अत्ता वै᳘ क्षत्रियो᳘ऽन्नं विड्यत्र᳘ वा अत्तुर᳘न्नं भू᳘यो भवति त᳘द्राष्ट्रꣳ स᳘मृद्धं भवति त᳘देधन्ते त᳘स्माँल्लोकंपृणा᳘ एव भू᳘यसीरु᳘पदध्यादि᳘त्येतद᳘हत᳘योर्वचोऽन्या᳘त्वेवा᳘तः स्थि᳘तिः ॥ १९ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

On the other hand, Tāṇḍya used to say "The bricks endowed with Yajus mantras are of royal class. It is the peasant class that fills the society (with plenty of food). The royal class is the eater and the peasantry is the food. When there is abundance of food for the consumer, that country is indeed rich and prosperous. Let him, therefore, have plenty of space-filling variety of bricks (piled up with the mantras starting with lokampṛṇā)". These were their respective views but the accepted practice is quite different (from either).
</details>


## २०

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

स᳘ एष᳘ पिता᳘ पुत्रो य᳘दे᳘षोऽग्निम᳘सृजत ते᳘नै᳘षोऽग्निः पिता य᳘देत᳘मग्निः᳘ स᳘मदधात्ते᳘नैत᳘स्याग्निः पिता य᳘देष᳘ देवान᳘सृजत ते᳘नैष᳘ देवा᳘नां पिता य᳘देतं᳘ देवाः᳘ सम᳘दधुस्ते᳘नैत᳘स्य देवाः᳘ पित᳘रः ॥ २० ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

Now that Father. Prajāpati, is (also) the son. In as much as he created Agni, thereby he is Agni's father; and in as much as Agni revived him, Agni is his father. Since he created the Devas he is their father and because the gods revived him, therefore, they are his fathers.
</details>


## २१

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

उभ᳘यꣳ हैत᳘द्भवति पिता᳘ च पुत्र᳘श्च प्रजा᳘पतिश्चाग्नि᳘श्चाग्नि᳘श्च प्रजा᳘पतिश्च प्रजा᳘पतिश्च देवा᳘श्च देवा᳘श्च प्रजा᳘पतिश्च य᳘ एवं वेद ॥ २१ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

Two-fold verily is this to those who understand (that). Father and son are Prajāpati and Agni; Agni and Prajāpati. (So too) Prajāpati and the gods; gods and Prajāpati.
</details>


## २२

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

स उ᳘पदधाति त᳘या देवत्येति वाग्वै सा᳘ देव᳘ताङ्गिरस्वदि᳘ति प्राणो वा अ᳘ङ्गिरा ध्रुवा᳘ सीदे᳘ति स्थिरा᳘ सीदे᳘त्येतदथो प्र᳘तिष्ठिता सीदेति वाचा चै᳘वैनमेत᳘त्प्राणे᳘न च चिनोति वाग्वा᳘ अग्निः प्राण इ᳘न्द्र ऐन्द्रा᳘ग्नोऽग्निर्यावानग्निर्या᳘वत्यस्य मा᳘त्रा ता᳘वतै᳘वैनमेत᳘च्चिनोतीन्द्राग्नी᳘ उ वै स᳘र्वे देवाः᳘ सर्वदेव᳘त्योऽग्निर्या᳘वानग्निर्या᳘वत्यस्य मा᳘त्रा ता᳘वतै᳘वैनमेत᳘च्चिनोति ॥ २२ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

He builds up with that deity. That deity is, doubtless, speech (vāk) which (is indicated by) the Ṛk mantra—starting with aṅgirasvad. Aṅgiras is no doubt the vital air. "(O Prāṇa!) be seated firm 'or' be seated well-entrenched". So with words (meaning) be well-settled and with the vital air, he builds up (the altar). Speech is Agni and vital air is Indra; and the fire (on the altar) is of both Indra and Agni. As great as the Agni is and as great as is his sway, by so much he thus builds him up. Moreover, Indra-Agni (duo) is all gods and Agni covers all the deities. Thus, as great as the Agni is and as great as is his sway, by so much he builds him up.
</details>


## २३

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

त᳘दाहुः क᳘स्मादस्या᳘ अग्नि᳘श्रीयत इ᳘ति य᳘त्र वै सा᳘ देव᳘ता व्य᳘स्रꣳसत त᳘दिमा᳘मेव र᳘सेना᳘नु᳘व्यक्षरत्तं य᳘त्र देवाः᳘ सम᳘स्कुर्वꣳस्त᳘देनमस्या᳘ एवा᳘धि स᳘मभरन्त्सैषैकैवेष्टकेयमे᳘वेय᳘ꣳ᳘ ह्यग्नि᳘रस्यै हि स᳘र्वोऽग्निश्चीय᳘ते᳘ सेयं च᳘तुःस्रक्तिर्दिशो᳘ ह्यस्यै हि स्रक्त᳘यस्त᳘स्माच्च᳘तुःस्रक्त᳘य इ᳘ष्टका भवन्ती᳘माꣳ ह्य᳘नु स᳘र्वा इ᳘ष्टकाः ॥ २३ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

Now they ask, "Why is the Agni (altar) built of this (earth)?" Surely, when that deity (Prajāpati) fell exhausted, he (in the form of his) life-sap flowed along this (earth) and when the gods revived him, they gathered (parts of his body) from this earth. This earth is alone that single brick; for, Agni is this (earth) indeed. That is why the whole Agni is built up on this (earth). Now, this earth is four-cornered. That is why all bricks are like that (four-cornered).
</details>


## २४

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

त᳘दाहुर्य᳘देवमे᳘केष्टकोऽथ कथं प᳘ञ्चेष्टक इ᳘तीत्यन्वेव᳘ प्रथमा᳘ मृन्मयी᳘ष्टका तद्यत्किञ्चा᳘त्र मृन्म᳘यमुपद᳘धात्ये᳘कैव से᳘ष्टका᳘थ य᳘त्पशुशीर्षा᳘ण्युपद᳘धाति सा᳘ पश्विष्टका᳘थ य᳘द्रुक्मपुरुषा᳘ उपद᳘धाति य᳘द्धिरण्यशकलैः᳘ प्रोक्ष᳘ति सा᳘ हिरण्यैष्टका᳘थ यत्स्रु᳘चा उपद᳘धाति य᳘दुलूखलमुसले याः᳘ समि᳘ध आद᳘धाति सा᳘ वान᳘स्पत्ये᳘ष्टका᳘थ य᳘त्पुष्करपर्ण᳘मुपदधाति य᳘त्कूर्मं यद्द᳘धि म᳘धु घृतं यत्कि᳘ञ्चात्रा᳘न्नमुपद᳘धाति सैवा᳘न्नं पञ्चमी᳘ष्टकैव᳘मु पञ्चे᳘ष्टकाः ॥ २४ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

They say, "If thus, there is only one brick, then how come he is five- bricked?" (Agni is referred to as Pañca citika or five-altared and so pañceṣṭakāḥscetavyāḥ or five bricks are to be built up). Now surely, the first brick made of clay is this (earth) and with whatever is made of clay he builds up (this altar) that is only one brick. And when he puts thereon the heads of the sacrificial animals, they form the bricks (made) of animals. When he puts on the gold plate and man and when he scatters gold files thereon, this is the golden brick. When he puts on two spoonfuls (of ghee) and puts on the mortar and pestle along with fire-wood sticks, that forms the wooden brick. And when he puts on a lotus petal, a tortoise, sour curds, honey, ghee and any other eatable, all that forms the fifth brick of food. Thus there are five bricks.
</details>


## २५

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

त᳘दाहुः कतर᳘त इ᳘ष्टकायाः शि᳘र इ᳘ति य᳘त उपस्पृ᳘श्य य᳘जुर्व᳘दती᳘ति है᳘क आहुः स᳘ स्वयमातृण्णा᳘या एवा᳘र्द्धादुपस्पृ᳘श्य य᳘जुर्वदेत्त᳘थो हास्यैताः स᳘र्वाः स्वयमातृण्णा᳘मभ्या᳘वृत्ता भवन्ती᳘ति न त᳘था कुर्याद᳘ङ्गानि वा᳘ अस्यैता᳘नि परूꣳषि यदि᳘ष्टका य᳘था वा अ᳘ङ्गे अङ्गे प᳘र्वन्पर्वञ्छि᳘रः कुर्या᳘त्तादृक्त᳘द्यो वाव᳘ चि᳘तेऽग्नि᳘र्निधीय᳘ते त᳘दे᳘वैता᳘साꣳ स᳘र्वासाꣳ शिरः ॥ २५ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

They now say "Which is the head of the brick?" "Where he touches it and chants the Yajus mantra". Some say thus: "On the one end of the naturally perforated (brick) alone, indeed should he chant a Yajus mantrawhile touching it". In this process, all those (bricks) of his get turned towards the naturally perforated one. One should not do like that. These bricks are the limbs of Agni; the joints of Agni. This would (chanting the Yajus while touching the brick) amount to make a head at each limb and at each joint. Because, that Agni which is installed on the altar is indeed the head of all these (limbs).
</details>


## २६

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

त᳘दाहुः क᳘ति पशवोऽग्ना उ᳘पधीयन्त इ᳘ति पञ्चे᳘ति न्वेव᳘ ब्रूयात्प᳘ञ्च ह्येता᳘न्पशू᳘नुपद᳘धाति ॥ २६ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

Here they ask, "How many sacrificial animals are laid upon the fire (altar)?" He should reply "Five", because he indeed lays five sacrificial animals.
</details>


## २७

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अ᳘थो ए᳘क इ᳘ति ब्रूयाद᳘विरि᳘तीयं वा अ᳘विरिय᳘ꣳ᳘ हीमाः स᳘र्वाः प्रजा अ᳘वतीय᳘मु वा᳘ अग्नि᳘रस्यै हि स᳘र्वोऽग्नि᳘श्चीय᳘ते त᳘स्मादे᳘क इ᳘ति ब्रूयाद᳘थो द्वावि᳘ति ब्रूयाद᳘वी इ᳘तीयं चासौ᳘ चेमे᳘ हीमाः स᳘र्वाः प्रजा अ᳘वतो य᳘न्मृदियं तद्यदा᳘पोऽसौ त᳘न्मृच्चा᳘पश्चे᳘ष्टका भवन्ति त᳘स्माद्द्वावि᳘ति ब्रूयात् ॥ २७ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

Or he may say, "One". "an ewe" (avi), for, an ewe is this earth, because (the earth) protects (av) all these creatures. This (earth) is indeed the Agni (fire-altar). Because the entire fire-altar is built up thereof. Hence he may say "One". Or else, he may say "Two". "Two sheep" for, this earth is one (ewe) and that (sky) is another. Then two protect (av) all creatures. What is as clay (in the brick) is of earth and what is as water (in the brick) is of the sky, and the bricks consist of clay and water (the two avis). Therefore, he may say "Two".
</details>


## २८

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अ᳘थ गौरि᳘ति ब्रूयादिमे वै᳘ लोका गौर्यद्धि कि᳘ञ्च ग᳘च्छतीमाꣳस्तं᳘ लोका᳘न्गच्छतीम उ लोका᳘ ए᳘षोऽग्नि᳘श्चितस्त᳘स्माद्गौरि᳘ति ब्रूयात् ॥ २८ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

Or he may say, "A cow". These worlds are indeed cow, for, whatever walks (gam) that moves, towards these worlds. This fire (altar) that is being built up also encompasses these worlds. Therefore, he may say "A cow".
</details>


## २९

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

त᳘दाहुः क᳘स्मै का᳘मायाग्नि᳘श्चीय᳘त इ᳘ति सुपर्णो᳘ मा भूत्वा दि᳘वं वहादि᳘ति है᳘क आहुर्न त᳘था विद्यादे᳘तद्वै᳘ रूपं कृत्वा᳘ प्राणाः᳘ प्रजा᳘पतिरभव᳘न्नेत᳘द्रूपं कृत्वा᳘ प्रजा᳘पतिर्देवा᳘नसृजतैत᳘द्रूपं᳘कृत्वा᳘ देवा᳘ अमृ᳘ता अभवꣳस्त᳘द्यदे᳘वैते᳘न प्राणा अभवन्य᳘त्प्रजा᳘पतिर्य᳘द्देवा᳘स्तदे᳘वैते᳘न भवति ॥ २९ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

To this they said, "For what purpose is this fire (altar) being built up". "(This Agni) having become a bird shall bear me to the heavens"—so say some. Let him not think so. (Because) by assuming that form, the vital airs became Prajāpati; by assuming that form Prajāpati created the gods and by assuming that form the gods became immortal. Therefore, what the vital airs became, what Prajāpati and what the gods became, that indeed, he (the sacrificer) will thereby become. (Second Brāhmaṇa Ends)
</details>
