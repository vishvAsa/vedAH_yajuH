## ०१

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अथा᳘तः प्रा᳘यश्चित्तीनां यद्य᳘श्वो व᳘डवाꣳ स्क᳘न्देद्वाय᳘व्यं प᳘योऽनुनि᳘र्वपेद्वायुर्वै रे᳘तसां विकर्ता᳘ प्राणो वै᳘ वायुः᳘ प्राणो हि रे᳘तसां विकर्ता रे᳘तसै᳘वास्मिꣳस्तद्रे᳘तो दधाति ॥ १ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

Now we take up the expiations - if the sacrificial horse is to mate with a mare, an additional milk oblation, he should offer to Vāyu. Vāyu, doubtless is the one who transforms the seed. Vāyu is indeed Prāṇa and Prāṇa is the one that transforms the seed. By means of seed he thus puts seed into it.
</details>


## ०२

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अ᳘थ य᳘दि स्रामो᳘ विन्दे᳘त्पौष्णं᳘ चरु᳘मनुनिर्वपेत्पूषा वै᳘ पशूना᳘मिष्टे स य᳘स्यैव᳘ पश᳘वो यः᳘ पशूनामि᳘ष्टे त᳘मे᳘वैत᳘त्प्रीणात्यगदो᳘ हैव᳘ भवति ॥ २ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

If the horse should contact some ailment, he should in addition offer a caru to Pūṣa. Pūṣa is the lord of the cattle. He thus pleases the one who is the lord of the cattle and it (horse) becomes free from the ailment.
</details>


## ०३

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अ᳘थ य᳘द्यक्षतामयो᳘ विन्दे᳘द्वैश्वानरं द्वा᳘दशकपालं भू᳘मिकपालं पुरोडा᳘शमनुनि᳘र्वपेदियं वै वैश्वानर᳘ इमा᳘मेवैत᳘त्प्रीणात्यगदो᳘ हैव᳘ भवति ॥ ३ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

If the horse becomes sick without any visible injury, he should offer, in addition, a Puroḍāśa in tweleve potsherds with earth used as potsherds, to Vaiśvānara. Vaiśvānara is this earth and thereby he pleases her and it (horse) gets free from sickness.
</details>


## ०४

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अ᳘थ य᳘द्यक्ष्यामयो᳘ विन्दे᳘त्सौर्यं᳘ चरु᳘मनुनि᳘र्वपेत्सू᳘र्यो वै᳘ प्रजा᳘नां च᳘क्षुर्यदा᳘ ह्ये᳘वैष उदेत्य᳘थेदꣳ स᳘र्वं चरति च᳘क्षुषै᳘वास्मिꣳस्तच्चक्षुर्दधाति स य᳘च्चरुर्भ᳘वति च᳘क्षुषा᳘ ह्यय᳘मात्मा च᳘रति ॥ ४ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

If the horse suffers from some eye-ailment, a caru to Sūrya should be prepared in addition. The Sun is indeed the eye of the living beings. When he rises, all these beings start moving. By means of eye, he endows it with eye in the fire of that caru. It is by means of the eye, this self (body) moves.
</details>


## ०५

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अ᳘थ य᳘द्युदके᳘ म्रिये᳘त वारुणं यवम᳘यं चरु᳘मनुनि᳘र्वपेद्व᳘रुणो वा᳘ एतं गृह्णा᳘ति योऽप्सु म्रिय᳘ते सा यै᳘ वैनं देव᳘ता गृह्णा᳘ति ता᳘मे᳘वैत᳘त्प्रीणाति᳘ सास्मै प्री᳘तान्य᳘मालं भाया᳘नुमन्यते तया᳘नुमतमा᳘लभते स य᳘द्यवम᳘यो भवति वरु᳘ण्याः हि य᳘वाः ॥ ५ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

If the horse were to die in water, he should offer a caru made of barley to Varuṇa, in addition. Because it is Varuṇa who captures that which dies in water. That deity which captures is thereby appeased. So pleased that deity approves of another horse for the purpose of immolation. Approved by that deity, he seizes another horse. The caru is of barley for, barley belongs to Varuṇa.
</details>


## ०६

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अ᳘थ य᳘दि न᳘श्येत्रिहविषमि᳘ष्टिमनुनि᳘र्वपेद्द्या᳘वा᳘पृथि᳘व्यमे᳘ककपालं पुरोडा᳘शं वाय᳘व्यं प᳘यः सौर्यं᳘ चरुं यद्वै कि᳘ञ्च न᳘श्यत्यन्त᳘रैव तदद्या᳘वापृथिवी᳘ नश्यति त᳘द्वायुरु᳘पवात्यादि᳘त्योऽभि᳘तपति᳘ नैता᳘भ्यो देव᳘ताभ्य ऋते कि᳘ञ्चन᳘ नश्यति᳘ सैषा पृ᳘थगेव᳘ नष्टवे᳘दनी स य᳘द्यस्या᳘प्यन्यन्न᳘श्येदेत᳘यैव᳘ यजेता᳘नु है᳘वैतद्विन्दत्य᳘थ य᳘द्यमित्रा अ᳘श्वं विन्दे᳘रन्य᳘दि वा म्रिये᳘त य᳘दि वा᳘प्स्व᳘न्यमानी᳘य प्रो᳘क्षेयुः᳘ सैव तत्र प्रा᳘यश्चित्तिः ॥ ६ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

If the horse were to get lost, let him, in addition, perform an Iṣṭi with three Havis. A Puroḍāśa in one potsherd to the heven and earth; a milk obla- tion to Vāyu ; and a caru to Sun; for, that which is lost is lost between heaven and earth; the wind blows upon it and the Sun shines upon it. Without (the involvement ) of these deities(Heaven, Earth, Vāyu and Sun) nothing gets lost. These offerings by themselves are efficacious in recovering the lost thing. So even if something else gets lost, let him perform this Iṣṭi and he is sure to find it. If his enemies were to capture the horse or if it were to die (on the ground) or in waters, let him procure another horse and consecrate it by sprinkling. This indeed is the expiation in that case. (Ninth Brāhmaṇa Ends.) (Chapter Three Ends.)
</details>
