स᳓र्वेषु वै᳓ लोके᳓षु मृत्य᳓वोऽन्वा᳓यत्तास्ते᳓भ्यो य᳓दा᳓हुतीर्न᳓ जुहुया᳓ल्लोके᳓ लोक एनं मृत्यु᳓र्विन्देद्य᳓न्मृत्युभ्य᳓ [[आ᳓हुतीर्जुहोति|आ᳓हुतीर्जुहो᳓ति]] लोके᳓ लोक [[एव|एव᳓]] मृत्यु᳓म᳓प जयति ॥

In all the worlds, Mṛtyu, the causes of death are inhere. If oblations are not offered to them, death will catch hold of him in each of the worlds. By offering oblations to Mṛtyu, he overcomes death in each world.

They now- “If he were to offer oblations to the causes of death saying to this Death-Svāhā, to this Death-Svāhā—he will be in the process, making en- emies of many (causes of) deaths and would ultimately give himself over to death.” So he offers only one oblation to one of them- “to Mṛtyu-Svāhā”. There is only one cause of death in the yonder world, namely hunger. Only that hunger in the yonder world, he conquers.

The second oblation he offers saying “to Brahmahatyā-Svāhā.” For, a killing other than that of a brahman is not Mṛtyu (a cause of death). This is the evident cause of death; namely, the killing of a brahmin. He thus over- comes the sure cause of death. This is what Muṇḍibha Audarya discovered as an expiation for killing a brahmin. When he offers oblation to Brahmahatyā, he pleases the Mṛtyu himself with the offering and thereby rendering himself absolutely worthy (fully immunised to sin), he effects a remedy for the killer of a brahmin. Hence at whosoever’s Aśvamedha this oblation is offered, if some- one among his subjects happens to kill a brahmin subsequently, he is provid- ing such a one (killer) with a remedy. (Sixth Brāhmaṇa Ends.)
