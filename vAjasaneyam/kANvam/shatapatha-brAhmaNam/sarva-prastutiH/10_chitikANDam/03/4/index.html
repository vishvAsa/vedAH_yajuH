Then he places the vālakhilyā bricks. The vālakhilyās are indeed the vital airs. Thus he endows him with vital airs. Why they are named vālakhilyās — uncultivated space between two furrowed land-pieces is called khila. These prāṇas (channels of breaths) are separated from each other only by the breadth of a horse’s hair. Since they are separated only by the breadth of a horse’s hair, they are called vālakhilyās.

He places seven of them in the front and seven in the back. Those seven which he places in the front are, no doubt, those prāṇas (vital air passages) in the front. They are now placed in it. This is as it were an ascent from here (the earth) but this earth is a base and gods did return to this earth, the base. “You are the leader, the king” — so saying they descended to that yonder world. “You are the one who guides; a guide” — so saying they came back to the aerial region. “You are steady and dharaṇī(holder) – so saying they returned to this terrestrial world. “You for longevity of life, you for vitality; you for agriculture, you for well-being” — they are four types of quadrupeds (animals). The animals are food and thus by means of these four type of animals, food, they — (gods) came back to this terrestrial world. In the same manner this Yajamāna, by these four types of animals, food, returns to this world.
