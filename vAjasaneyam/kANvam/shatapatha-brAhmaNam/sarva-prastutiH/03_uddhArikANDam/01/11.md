## ०१

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

स वा अ᳘ग्रेणाहवनी᳘यं परी᳘त्य जघ᳘नेन वा गा᳘र्हपत्यम᳘न्तरेणेत्वो᳘पविशति स यद᳘न्तरेणे᳘त्वोपविश᳘ति न᳘ ह वै᳘ देवा᳘ मनु᳘ष्यं विदुरय᳘मस्मा᳘कमस्य वय᳘ꣳ᳘ स्म इ᳘ति त᳘ꣳ᳘ हैवम᳘न्तरेण य᳘न्तं विदुरय᳘मस्मा᳘कमस्य वय᳘ꣳ᳘ स्म इ᳘ति त᳘स्मै ह तं का᳘मꣳ स᳘मर्धयति य᳘स्मै का᳘माया᳘न्तरेणोपविशति ॥ १ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

Having gone around in front (ahead) of the Āhavanīya and behind the Gārhapatya, he goes and sits in between them. (The reason) why he sits having gone up in between (the two fires) (is this): Gods do not indeed know the man (as) “This person is ours and we are his”. They verily know him who passes between (them) thus, “This person belongs to us; we are his”. To him indeed does he fulfil that desire, for the sake of which desire he sits in between.
</details>


## ०२

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अ᳘थ य᳘द्वेवा᳘न्तरेणोपविशत्य᳘पहतपाप्मा᳘नो वै᳘ देवा अ᳘नपहतपाप्मानो मनु᳘ष्यास्त᳘स्य हैवम᳘न्तरेण य᳘तोऽग्नी᳘ पाप्मा᳘नम᳘पहतस्तं᳘ पाप्मा᳘ नाभ्य᳘त्येति सोऽपहतपाप्मा भवति ॥ २ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

And (the reason) why he sits in between (the two fires) (is this): Indeed the gods are free from evil; men do not have their evils removed. The two fires destroy the evil of this (sacrificer) who moves thus in between. Evil does not reach (touch) him. He becomes free from evil.
</details>


## ०३

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अ᳘थ य᳘द्वेवा᳘न्तरेणोपविश᳘त्येषा वै नौः स्व᳘र्ग्या य᳘दग्निहोत्रं त᳘स्या एत᳘स्या नावः᳘ स्व᳘र्ग्यायाः क्षीर᳘होतैव᳘ नावाजास्ताम᳘तः प्रा᳘चीम᳘भ्यजति त᳘स्या अ᳘तोऽधिरो᳘हणं तामतो᳘ऽधिरोहति ॥ ३ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

And (the reason) why he sits in between (the two fires) (is this): This thing viz., Agnihotra is indeed the ship that leads to heaven. Of this ship that would lead to heaven the offerer of milk (the Agnihotrin) himself is the helmsman. Thence, he steers the ship eastward. Therefore. is the boarding of that (ship) (done). Hence he boards her.
</details>


## ०४

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

स य᳘द्ध दक्षिणत᳘ उपा᳘सीत य᳘था पारा᳘र्थं नौ᳘र्जह्या᳘देव᳘ꣳ᳘ ह तद᳘थ य᳘द्धुते पु᳘नरै᳘ति त᳘त्स्वर्गे᳘ लोके प्र᳘तितिष्ठत्येतद्वै᳘ स्वर्ग᳘स्य लोक᳘स्य द्वा᳘रꣳ स यद᳘न्तरेणोपविश᳘ति त᳘त्स्वर्गं᳘ लोकं प्र᳘पद्यते ॥ ४ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

If he should sit (after entering) from the south, it would be indeed as a ship might leave for the other shore. And as he returns after the offering has been made, by that he establishes himself in the world of heaven. This is indeed the gateway to the heavenly world. In that he sits after having gone in between (the two fires), by that he reaches the world of heaven.
</details>


## ०५

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अथ यद्ध दक्षिणत उपासीत यथा द्वारेण प्रतिपित्सेतैवः ह तदथ यद्धुते पुनरैति तत्स्वर्गे लोके प्र᳘तितिष्ठति ॥ ५ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

And, if he were to sit (entering) from the south, it would be as one would wish to reach it through the gate. And by (the fact) that he returns after the offering has been made, he remains established in the world of heaven.
</details>


## ०६

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

एष वाव᳘ प्रजा᳘पतिर्यज्ञो य᳘दग्निहोत्रं त᳘स्यैतस्य प्रजा᳘पतेर्यज्ञ᳘स्य य᳘जमान एव᳘ प्राणो या᳘व᳘द्ध्येव य᳘जमानः प्राणेन प्रा᳘णिति ता᳘वदेव᳘ जुहोत्य᳘थ यदा᳘ प्रा᳘णोऽपक्रा᳘मत्य᳘थ व्य᳘वच्छिद्यते स यद᳘न्तरेणे᳘त्वोपविश᳘ति प्राण᳘ एव त᳘द्भूत्वा प्र᳘विशत्य᳘थ य᳘द्धुते पु᳘नरै᳘त्युदान᳘ एव त᳘द्भूत्वा पु᳘नरै᳘ति स वा अयं पुरुषोऽस्मै᳘ प्राणाया᳘नार्त्तिं कामयत एव᳘ꣳ᳘ हास्मा एता᳘ देव᳘ता अ᳘नार्त्तिं कामयन्ते स ह᳘ नर्च्छत्यक्षीय᳘ꣳ᳘ ह जयति य᳘ एव᳘मेतद्वे᳘द स᳘ य᳘दास्मा᳘ल्लोकात्प्रैत्य᳘थास्यैत᳘ ए᳘वाग्न᳘यः प्राणा᳘ भवन्त्यक्षीय᳘ꣳ᳘ वा᳘ अमृ᳘तमेते᳘ प्राणास्तस्य हाक्षीय᳘ꣳ᳘ सुकृतं᳘ भवत्यक्षीय᳘ꣳ᳘ ह जयति य᳘ एव᳘मेतद्वेद ॥ ६ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

Agnihotra indeed is this Prajāpati, the sacrifice. The sacrificer himself is the life- energy (prāṇa) of this Prajāpati, the sacrifice. As long verily as the sacrificer breathes with his life-energy, till then he makes the offerings. And when life departs, then it (the offering/Agnihotra) is cut off. When he sits after reaching in between (the two fires), then becoming vital energy he enters it. And when he returns after the offering has been made, then he returns verily after-having become udāna (the inward breathing). This person indeed desires ‘absence of affliction’ for this life (of the sacrificer). Thus, these gods desire freedom from affliction for this (sacrificer). He who knows this to be thus, does not fail in faculties; he indeed conquers fully (undiminishingly). When he departs from this world, then these very fires become his vital energies (breaths). What does not diminish is indeed immortal. He who knows this to be so, his merit becomes undiminished and he conquers indeed fully (undiminishingly). (Eleventh Brāhmaṇa Ends)
</details>
