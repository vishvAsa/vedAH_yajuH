## ०१

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

स वा᳘ आहवनी᳘यमि᳘त्वा स्रुवे᳘णै᳘वाज्यस्थाल्या᳘ एता आ᳘हुतीर्जुहोत्याप᳘ये स्वा᳘हा स्वाप᳘ये स्वा᳘हापिजा᳘य स्वा᳘हा क्र᳘तवे स्वा᳘हा । व᳘सवे स्वा᳘हाहर्प᳘तये स्वा᳘हा । अ᳘ह्ने मुग्धा᳘य स्वा᳘हा मुग्धा᳘य वैनꣳशिना᳘य स्वा᳘हा विनꣳशि᳘न आन्त्यायना᳘य स्वाहा᳘न्त्याय भौवना᳘य स्वा᳘हा भु᳘वनस्य प᳘तये स्वाहा᳘धिपतये स्वाहे᳘त्येता द्वा᳘दशा᳘हुतीर्जुहोति प्रजा᳘पतिं वा᳘ एष उ᳘ज्जयति यो᳘ वाजपे᳘येन य᳘जते द्वादश वै मा᳘साः संवत्सर᳘स्य संवत्सरो वै᳘ प्रजा᳘पतिस्त᳘त्प्रजा᳘पतिमु᳘ज्जयति ॥ १ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

He goes to the Āhavanīya and with the sruva, he offers these (following) offerings from the ājyasthālī (pot for melting butter) “To the one obtained (Āpaye), Svāhā; to the one well-obtained (Svāpaye), Svāhā; to the water-borne, Svāhā; to the sacrifice or intention, Svāhā; to the Vasu, Svāhā; to the lord of the days (i.e. the year), Svāhā; to the day which knows no evil, Svāhā; to the innocent who is favourable to wrong doers, Svāhā; to the one born last to a wrong-doer, Svāhā; to the youngest son born of the universe, Svāhā; to the lord of the universe, Svāhā; to the over-lord, Svāhā”. Thus he makes twelve offerings. He who performs Vājapeya is winning Prajāpati himself. There are twelve months for a year and Prajāpati is the year. Therefore he wins Prajāpati.
</details>


## ०२

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अ᳘थैनमेताः षट् कॢ᳘प्तीर्वाच᳘यति वा जुहो᳘ति वा समान᳘ एव ब᳘न्धुर्य᳘दि च वाच᳘यति य᳘दि च जुहोत्या᳘युर्यज्ञे᳘न कल्पतां प्राणो᳘ यज्ञे᳘न कल्पतां च᳘क्षुर्यज्ञे᳘न कल्पताꣳ श्रो᳘त्रं यज्ञे᳘न कल्पताम् । पृष्ठं᳘ यज्ञे᳘न कल्पतां यज्ञो᳘ यज्ञे᳘न कल्पतामि᳘ति प्रजा᳘पतिं वा᳘ एष उ᳘ज्जयति यो᳘ वाजपे᳘येन य᳘जते षड्वा᳘ ऋत᳘वस्संवत्सर᳘स्य संवत्सरो वै᳘ प्रजा᳘पतिस्त᳘त्प्रजा᳘पतिमु᳘ज्जयत्य᳘थ य᳘देनं कॢ᳘प्तीर्वाचय᳘ति कल्पयि᳘त्वै᳘वैनं तदु᳘ज्जयति ॥ २ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

Then he makes him utter or make offerings with these six kḷptis (mantras having a verb from the root kḷp). Whether he utters or offers with them, in either case, the significance is the same. (They are) “May my life prosper through (this) sacrifice; let my vital air prosper through (this) sacrifice; let my eye prosperthrough (this) sacrifice; let my ear prosper through (this) sacrifice; let the backside of my body prosper through (this) sacrifice; may the sacrifice prosper through (this) sacrifice”. He who performs Vājapeya is winning Prajāpati. There are six seasons in a year; and Prajāpati is the year. By making him utter these kḷptis, he wins Prajāpati. Having strengthened (his sense-organs) he wins Prajāpati.
</details>


## ०३

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

स वा᳘ ए᳘षोऽष्टा᳘श्रिर्यू᳘पो भवत्यष्टा᳘क्षरा वै᳘ गाय᳘त्र्यग्नि᳘रुवै᳘ गायत्री᳘ गायत्र᳘च्छन्दो᳘ ह्यग्नि᳘रग्नि᳘रु वै स᳘र्वा देव᳘तास्ते᳘न देवलोकमु᳘ज्जयति ॥ ३ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

The yūpa is eight-cornered; for, Gāyatrī is eight-syllabled and Gāyatrī is Agni, because, Agni has Gāyatrī as its metre. Agni means all the gods. So thereby, he wins the divine world.
</details>


## ०४

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अथा᳘तीक्ष्णाग्रो यू᳘पो भवति ग᳘र्तस्य रूपं᳘ पितृदेव᳘त्यो वै ग᳘र्तस्ते᳘न पितृलोकमु᳘ज्जयति ॥ ४ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

Then the yūpa has a non-sharp (blunt) top. It has the shape of a pit (at the top); pit has the pitṛs as its deity. Therefore he wins the world of pitṛs.
</details>


## ०५

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अ᳘थ गोधू᳘माश्चषालभाजनं᳘ भवन्ति पु᳘रुषो वा᳘ अयं ने᳘दिष्ठं प्रजा᳘पतेः᳘ सोऽय᳘मत्वक्पु᳘रुष एता᳘ उ वा ओ᳘षधीनां ने᳘दिष्ठाः पु᳘रुषस्य य᳘द्गोधू᳘मा न᳘ ह्येते᳘षां त्वग᳘स्ति न पु᳘रुषस्य ते᳘न मनुष्यलोकमु᳘ज्जयति ॥ ५ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

It (the pit on the top of the yūpa) is like a cup made of wheat dough. This man is nearest to Prajāpati and he is skinless. And among the plants, wheat comes nearest to man. They (wheat) have no skin, nor has the man. By that he wins the world of men.
</details>


## ०६

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

स᳘ सप्तदश᳘भिर्वा᳘सोभिर्वेष्टितो᳘ वा वि᳘नद्धो वा भवति सप्तदशो वै᳘ प्रजा᳘पतिस्त᳘स्मात्सप्तदश᳘भिर्वा᳘सोभिर्वेष्टितो᳘ वा वि᳘नद्धो वा भवति सप्त᳘दशारत्निर्वाव᳘ भवति सप्तदशो हि᳘ प्रजा᳘पतिस्त᳘त्प्रजा᳘पतिमु᳘ज्जयति ॥ ६ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

That (yūpa) is wound or bound by seventeen pieces of cloth; for, Prajāpati is seventeenfold. So it is covered or bound by seventeen pieces of cloth. It (yūpa) also measures seventeen aratnis (measures by hand). Prajāpati is seventeenfold. So he wins Prajāpati.
</details>


## ०७

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अ᳘थ य᳘त्र यू᳘पꣳ रोक्ष्यन्भ᳘वति तत्प᳘त्नीमुदा᳘नयति ता᳘मुदानेष्य᳘न्त: कौशं᳘ वा च᳘ण्डातकमभिप᳘रिधापयन्ति कौशं᳘ वा वासो᳘ऽस्ति वा᳘ अमेध्यं प᳘त्न्या यद᳘वाङ्ना᳘भेः᳘ सेदं प्रा᳘ची यज्ञं᳘ प्रसत्स्य᳘न्ती भवति पवि᳘त्रमु वै मे᳘ध: कुशास्त᳘थो पवि᳘त्रपूतैवैतन्मे᳘ध्या प्रा᳘ची यज्ञं प्र᳘सजति ॥ ७ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

Now, just before he climbs the yūpa, they bring the wife (of the Yajamāna). While taking her there, they make her wrapped in a garment made of kuśa grass or in a caṇḍātaka (a skirt covering half the thighs) made of kuśa grass, over the cloth (already being worn by her since the initiation). Let it be a garment of grass (to wrap over) now. That part of her body below the naval is impure (unfit for sacrificial occasion). She is now advancing to propitiate the sacrifice. The sacrifice is pure, so too are the kuśa grass. She, this much purified by the pure (kuśa garment), advances forward to propitiate this sacrifice.
</details>


## ०८

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अ᳘थ निःश्रयणी᳘कृता᳘ भवति त᳘या यू᳘पꣳ रोहति स इत्यु᳘दङ् रोहेत्तद्धै᳘क इ᳘ति दक्षिणा᳘रोहयन्ति त᳘दु त᳘था न᳘ कुर्यादु᳘दगेव᳘ रोहेदुदीची वै᳘ मनु᳘ष्याणां दिक्सा य᳘जमानस्य त᳘स्मादु᳘दगेव᳘ रोहयेत् ॥ ८ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

Then the wooden scaffold (ladder) is set ready. With that he ascends the yūpa. In this manner, he ascends northwards. Some climb southwards. That is not to be done so. One should climb only northwards, for the north is the direction of humans and it is (the quarter) that belongs to the Yajamāna. So he climbs only northwards.
</details>


## ०९

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

स यू᳘पꣳ रोक्ष्य᳘न्जायामा᳘मन्त्रयते जा᳘य ए᳘हि᳘ स्वो रोहावे᳘ति र᳘हावे᳘ति जाया प्र᳘त्याह स य᳘देवं᳘ जाया᳘मामन्त्र᳘यतेऽर्धो वा᳘ अस्यैष᳘ आत्म᳘नो यज्जा᳘यार्धो हि वा᳘ अस्यैष᳘ आत्म᳘नस्त᳘स्माद्या᳘वदेकाकी भ᳘वति नैव ता᳘वद्बहु᳘र्भवति न प्र᳘जायते᳘ऽकृत्स्नो हि भ᳘वत्य᳘सर्वो᳘ऽथ य᳘दैव᳘ जायां᳘ विन्दते᳘ऽथैव᳘ बहु᳘र्भवत्य᳘थ प्र᳘जायते कृत्स्नो हि भ᳘वति स᳘र्वः स्वर्ग᳘मु वा᳘ इदं᳘ लोक᳘ꣳ रोक्ष्य᳘न्भवति᳘ योऽस्यैष᳘ जितः᳘ स्वर्गो᳘ लोकस्तद्य᳘दिद᳘मस्यार्ध᳘मात्म᳘नस्ते᳘न संप᳘द्यते ते᳘न संप᳘द्यैतां᳘ जि᳘तिमभ्यु᳘त्क्रामति᳘ योऽस्यैव᳘ जितः᳘ स्वर्गो᳘ लोकस्त᳘थास्येह᳘ नात्म᳘नः कि᳘ञ्चन᳘ हीयते ॥ ९ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

While about to climb the yūpa, he addresses his wife, "Come wife, let us ascend to the sky". She replies "Let us ascend". Now, why he addresses thus the wife is this—she who is the wife is one half of his own self. She is indeed one half of his self. That is why, as long as he is alone, he does not become many, does not procreate. He is deficient, incomplete. And only when he gets a wife, then only he becomes many, procreates; becomes whole and complete. Climbing this (yūpa), he feigns to be climbing to the heavenly world; because he has won the heavenly world (by the Vājapeya). That which is his half (wife), with that he unites. Having united with that (half) he attains that victory and that amounts to having won the heavenly world. Nothing of his is left over (here).
</details>


## १०

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

स᳘ रोहति प्रजा᳘पतेः प्रजा᳘ अभूमे᳘ति प्रजा᳘पते᳘र्ह्येष᳘ प्रजा भ᳘वति यो᳘ वाज᳘पेयेन य᳘जते त᳘स्मादाह प्रजा᳘पतेः प्रजा᳘ अभूमे᳘ति᳘ स्वर्देवा अगन्मे᳘ति गोधू᳘मानभि᳘मृशति स्व᳘र्ह्येष᳘ देवानि᳘ति यो᳘ वाज᳘पेयेन य᳘जते स य᳘द्गोधू᳘मानभिमृशत्य᳘न्नं वा᳘ एष उ᳘ज्जयति यो᳘ वाजपे᳘येन य᳘जते एतद्वै᳘` प्रत्य᳘क्षम᳘न्नं यद्गो᳘धूमा᳘स्त᳘देतां ग᳘तिं गत्वा᳘ योऽस्यैष जितः᳘ स्वर्गो᳘ लोको य᳘देतद᳘न्नमुज्ज᳘यति ते᳘न स᳘ꣳ᳘स्पृशते त᳘दात्म᳘नि कुरुते त᳘दात्म᳘नि धत्ते ते᳘नोद᳘रमु᳘पस्पृशत्य᳘त्र ह्य᳘न्नं प्रतिति᳘ष्ठति ॥ १० ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

He ascends (thinking) "We have become Prajāpati's children". He who performs Vājapeya does indeed become Prajāpati's child. He touches the wheat (on the top of the yūpa, saying) "We have reached to the light, O gods!" He who performs Vājapeya indeed, reaches the light. And why he touches the wheat is because he who performs Vājapeya wins food. Having gone to that (supreme) state and having won the heavenly world, he has won this food. So he contacts it and puts it into himself, owns it in himself. With that (wheat dough) he smears his belly because it is there (in the belly) that food settles.
</details>


## ११

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अ᳘थ यू᳘पाग्रꣳ शिरो᳘ऽतिहरति ते᳘न देवलोकमु᳘ज्जयति त᳘थास्याः᳘ प्रत्य᳘वरुळ्हं भवति यू᳘पाऽग्रꣳ शि᳘रोऽतिहृ᳘त्य जपत्यस्मे᳘ वो अस्त्विन्द्रिय᳘मस्मे᳘ तृम्ण᳘मुत क्र᳘तुः । अस्मे व᳘र्चाꣳसि सन्तु व इ᳘ति प्रजा᳘पतिं वा᳘ एष उ᳘ज्जयति यो᳘ वाजपे᳘येन य᳘जते स᳘र्वं वा᳘ इदं᳘ प्रजा᳘पतिर्य᳘दिमे᳘ लोका य᳘दिमा दि᳘शस्त᳘त्प्रजा᳘पतिमुज्जि᳘त्यास्य स᳘र्वस्येन्द्रियं᳘ वी᳘र्यꣳ सं᳘वृङ्क्ते स यः᳘ संवृङ्क्ते त᳘स्मात्सम्राणाम् ॥ ११ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

He then rises by (the measure of) his head above the yūpa. By that he wins the world of the gods. Raising the head, he chants "Ours be your power, ours be your manliness and your intelligence; ours be your glories". He who performs Vājapeya does win Prajāpati and Prajāpati is all these; all these worlds, all these quarters. So he having won Prajāpati, he gains the power and glory of everything. He who thus acquires (everything) is called the Samrāt (emperor).
</details>


## १२

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अ᳘थैनमूषपुटै᳘रनू᳘दस्यन्त्य᳘न्नं वा᳘ एष उ᳘ज्जयति यो᳘ वाजपे᳘येन य᳘जतेऽस्या᳘मु वा᳘ इदम᳘न्नमस्या᳘ꣳ᳘ हीद᳘मन्नाद्यं पश᳘वो वा ऊ᳘षा एतद्वै᳘ प्रत्य᳘क्षम᳘न्नं य᳘त्पश᳘वस्त᳘स्मादूषपुटा᳘ भवन्ति ॥ १२ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

They throw at him packets of salt. He who performs Vājapeya wins food. In this salt, all food is contained; in this all the eaters of food. For salt means cattle and cattle is visible food. From them these salt packets come into being.
</details>


## १३

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अश्वत्थपलाशैरु᳘पनद्धा भवन्ति मारुतो वा᳘ अश्वत्थो य᳘दश्वत्थे ति᳘ष्ठत इ᳘न्द्रो मरु᳘त उपा᳘मन्त्रयते ते᳘न मारु᳘तोऽश्वत्थो वि᳘शो वै᳘ मरुतो᳘ऽन्नमु वै वि᳘शस्त᳘स्मादश्वत्थपलाशैरु᳘पनद्धा भवन्ति ॥ १३ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

These (salts) are packed in the leaves of Aśvattha, Aśvattha belongs to the Maruts. (Once) Indra addressed the Maruts which were staying on the Aśvattha tree. Therefore Aśvattha belongs to the Maruts. The Maruts are the viṭs (peasants) and peasants are food. Therefore these salts are wrapped in Aśvattha leaves.
</details>


## १४

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

वि᳘श्या अनू᳘दस्यन्त्य᳘न्नꣳ हि वि᳘शः सप्त᳘दश भवन्ति सप्तदशो हि᳘ प्रजा᳘पतिस्स य᳘दे᳘वैतद᳘न्नमुज्ज᳘यति ते᳘नै᳘वैनमेतत्स᳘ꣳस्पर्शयति ते᳘न स᳘मर्धयति त᳘दस्मिन्दधाति ॥ १४ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

Along with the peasants, they (salts) are thrown up. Viṭs are food. They (packets of salt) are seventeen and Prajāpati is seventeenfold. Because he wins this food, he causes that (food in the form of the salt) to touch it (the yūpa). By that he enriches. He puts it into it.
</details>


## १५

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अ᳘थ पृथिवीं᳘ प्रत्यवे᳘क्षमाणो जपति न᳘मो मात्रे᳘ पृथिव्या इ᳘ति यत्र᳘ ह वा अ᳘ग्रे बृ᳘हस्प᳘तिरेतं यज्ञं᳘ प्रजा᳘पतिमुदजयत्त᳘दास्याः᳘ पृथिव्या᳘ विभयां᳘ चकार᳘ प्रेव वा᳘ अह᳘मस्या᳘च्च्योषी᳘ति य᳘ इमं᳘ यज्ञं᳘ प्रजा᳘पतिमुद᳘जैषं यद्वै᳘ मेयं᳘ नावधून्वीते᳘तीय᳘मु ह वै᳘ पृथि᳘व्येत᳘स्माद्विभयां᳘ चकार महद्वा᳘ अयं प्रा᳘पदि᳘ति य᳘ इमं᳘ यज्ञं᳘ प्रजा᳘पतिमुद᳘जैषीद्यद्वै᳘ मायं᳘ नावदृणीयादि᳘ति त᳘थै᳘वै त᳘त्संज्ञा᳘मकुरुत न हि᳘ माता᳘ पुत्र᳘ꣳ᳘ हिन᳘स्ति न᳘ पुत्रो᳘ मात᳘रम् ॥ १५ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

Then looking at the earth, he chants "Homage be to the Mother Earth!" On that earlier occasion when Bṛhaspati won this sacrifice, namely Prajāpati, he became afraid of this earth (thinking) "I have swayed away from her (the earth) by my winning this sacrifice, that is Prajāpati. I fear lest she may shake me off". The earth got afraid of him (Bṛhaspati) "Something great surely, hath this person become by his winning this sacrifice, that is, Prajāpati. I fear lest he may despise me". (Bṛhaspati) came to an understanding with her (earth) "The mother would not harm the son, nor the son would, the mother".
</details>


## १६

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

त᳘थो वा᳘ एष᳘ एत᳘दस्याः᳘ पृथिव्या᳘ बिभेति᳘ प्रेव वा᳘ अह᳘मस्या᳘च्योषी᳘ति य᳘ इमं᳘ यज्ञं᳘ प्रजा᳘पतिमुद᳘जैषं यद्वै᳘ मेयं᳘ नावधून्वीते᳘तीय᳘मु ह वै᳘ पृथि᳘व्येत᳘स्माद्बिभेति महद्वा᳘ अयं प्रा᳘पदि᳘ति य᳘ इमं᳘ यज्ञं᳘ प्रजा᳘पतिमुद᳘जैषीद्यद्वै᳘ मायं᳘ नावधूणीयादि᳘ति त᳘थै᳘वैतत्संज्ञां᳘ कुरुते न हि᳘ माता᳘ पुत्र᳘ꣳ हिन᳘स्ति न᳘ पुत्रो᳘ मात᳘रम् ॥ १६ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

In like manner this one (Yajamāna) gets afraid of this earth (thinking) "I have swayed away from her by winning this sacrifice, that is, Prajāpati. I fear lest she may shake me off". The earth gets afraid of him (Yajamāna) "Something great surely, hath this person become by his winning this sacrifice, that is Prajāpati. I fear lest he may despise me". He (Yajamāna) comes to an understanding with her (earth) "The mother would not harm the son nor the son would, the mother".
</details>


## १७

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अ᳘थ हि᳘रण्यम᳘भ्यवरोहत्या᳘युर्वा᳘ अमृ᳘तꣳ हि᳘रण्यमा᳘युष्ये᳘वैनमेत᳘दमृ᳘तेऽन्ततः प्र᳘तितिष्ठति ॥ १७ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

He then descends, trampling upon a piece of gold. Gold is immortal life and so he ultimately gets established in immortal life.
</details>


## १८

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अ᳘थास्मा एतामौ᳘दुम्बरीमासन्दीं नि᳘दधत्य᳘ग्रेण हविर्धा᳘नं जघ᳘नेनाहवनी᳘यमुपरिष᳘द्यं वा᳘ एष᳘ जयत्यन्तरिक्षस᳘द्यं यो᳘ वाजपे᳘येन य᳘जते स य᳘देव ज᳘यत्युपरिष᳘द्यमन्तरिक्षस᳘द्यं त᳘दे᳘वैनमेतत्प्रा᳘पयति त᳘देनमुपर्या᳘सीनमध᳘स्ताद्वि᳘श इमाः᳘ प्रजा उ᳘पासत औ᳘दुंबरी भवत्य᳘न्नं वा ऊ᳘र्गुदुंब᳘रस्त᳘स्मादौ᳘दुंबरी भवति ॥ १८ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

Then they bring a seat of Udumbara wood for him (and place it) in front of the Havirdhāna and behind the Āhavanīya. He who performs Vājapeya gains a seat in the upper level, that is, a seat in the aerial region. His winning the upper seat, that is the seat in the aerial region entitles him for this (seat of Udumbara at a higher level). Him who is thus seated above, these commonfolk (prajā) seated below attend upon. (This seat) is of Udumbara wood; for Udumbarais sustenance or food. Hence it is of Udumbara wood.
</details>


## १९

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

तां᳘ बस्ताजिनेना᳘स्तृणाति प्रजा᳘पतिं वा᳘ एष उ᳘ज्जयति यो᳘ वाजपे᳘येन य᳘जत ऋषभो वै᳘ पशूनां᳘ प्रजा᳘पतिर᳘त्र वै᳘ प्रजा᳘पतिर्ने᳘दिष्ठो य᳘दजा᳘सु य᳘दजर्षभे स य᳘मे᳘वैत᳘त्प्रजा᳘पतिमुज्ज᳘यति ते᳘नै᳘वैनमेतत्स᳘ꣳ᳘स्पर्शयति ते᳘न स᳘मर्धयति त᳘स्माद्वस्ताजिनेना᳘स्तृणाति ॥ १९ ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

He covers it with a goat-skin. He who performs Vājapeya is winning Prajāpati. Prajāpati is the bull among (she) animals. Here in the case of the she- goats, the he-goat is the bull and Prajāpati is nearest to that Ajarsabha (bull among goats). That with which he wins Prajāpati, by that itself he makes it touch and enriches it with that. Therefore he covers it with a goat-skin.
</details>


## २०

<details open><summary>मूलम्</summary>

स आ᳘स्तृणाती᳘यं ते राळि᳘ति राज्य᳘मे᳘वास्मा एत᳘त्करोति राज्य᳘मेनं प्रा᳘पयत्य᳘थैनं बाहुगृहीतम᳘धिनयति य᳘न्तासि य᳘मन इ᳘ति यन्ता᳘रमे᳘वैनं य᳘मनमासां प्रजा᳘नां करोत्यथो᳘पवेशयति ध्रु᳘वोऽसि धरु᳘ण इ᳘ति ध्रुव᳘मे᳘वैनं धरु᳘णमेषु᳘ लोके᳘षु करोति कृष्यै क्षे᳘माय रय्यै पो᳘षाये᳘ति साध᳘व इ᳘त्ये᳘वैत᳘दाह ॥ २० ॥
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

He covers it (saying) "This is thy kingship"-whereby he bestows kingdom on him, he makes him get a kingdom. Then holding him (the Yajamāna) by hand, leads him down (saying) "Thou art the controller (of all), you are the ruler". He (thereby) makes him the controller and ruler of all these subjects. Then he makes him seated (saying) "Thou art firm and steadfast". Thereby he makes him firm and steadfast. "These worlds (he says) I make you sit for tilling, for protecting (what is earned), for wealth and for growth". He only means 'for welfare'. (Second Brāhmaṇa Ends)
</details>
