Then he yokes the left one (saying) "Become possessed of the wind's speed, O horse!, when thou art yoked"—thereby he renders him as swift as the wind; as swift as the thought. "Be thou possessed of the beauty of Indra's right (steed)". Just as Indra's right (steed) is prosperous, so you be for this Yajamāna to prosper—is what he says. "Let the Maruts that know everything, yoke thee; let Tvaṣṭā impart speed unto your legs"—for, the animals belong to Tvaṣṭā. He whose are the animals, he is the master of those animals. In human practice, the left one is yoked first, but in divine practice, it is this way (the right one first).

He makes the horses smell it (caru). (He says) "Ye horses, the winners of vāja"—vāja means food. So he says, 'winners of food'. (He says 'Ye who advance proceed forward, to the food'. So he only puts vigour into them. (He says) "This food, this sacrifice, namely Prajāpati—let me win". That is how he wins this food, this sacrifice, namely Prajāpati.
