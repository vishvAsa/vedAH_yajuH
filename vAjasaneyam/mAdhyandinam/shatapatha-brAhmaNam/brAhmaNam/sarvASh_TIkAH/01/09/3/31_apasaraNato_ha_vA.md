<details><summary>Eggeling</summary>

11. By (or, from) the escape (of the enemies) [^egg_626] indeed the conquering gods formerly gained first the sky, and then this aërial expanse; and thereupon

they drove their enemies away from this (earth), whence there was no escape.

[^egg_626]: 269:3 Apasaraṇataḥ; i.e. by allowing the enemies to escape, viz.; first from the sky to the air, and then from the air to the earth. It also, however, has the meaning of 'from escape;' that is, the gods drove the enemies to the earth, whence there was no escape for them.
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

At first the conquering gods conquered because of the moving away (of their enemies). At first (they conquered) the sky itself, then the aerial region and then they pushed away their rivals from this (earth), without (allowing them to) escape.
</details>

<details><summary>हरिस्वामी</summary>

**अपसरणतो ह** । जितापसरणेन जितवन्तो द्युलोकाख्यम्, अस्माल्लोकादपसृत्य असुरान् हतवन्तो देवाः । द्वितीयञ्च फलोपभोगानन्तरं प्रतिष्ठायां प्रच्युतानां स्वर्गाल्लोकान्निरुपद्रवात् । ततश्च यदितः पराचीनं क्रमेत, तदेतां गतिं गत्वा 'प्रतिष्ठायां प्रतितिष्ठति' ॥ ११ ॥
</details>
