<details><summary>Eggeling</summary>

5. There are twenty-one sacrificial stakes, all of them twenty-one cubits long. The central one [^egg_968] is of rajjudāla [^egg_969] wood;

[^egg_968]: 373:1 That is, the so-called 'agnishṭḥa,' 'standing by (or opposite) the (Āhavanīya) fire.' Cp. p. 301, note 1.

[^egg_969]: 373:2 The rajjudāla (or rājjudāla, Sāy. on Taitt. Br. III, 8, 19, 1) or 'sleshmātaka' is the Cordia Myxa or C. latifolia, from the bark of which (according to Stewart and Brandis, Forest Flora of N.W. and Centr. India) ropes (rajju) are made, whence doubtless the above, as well as its scientific name, is derived; whilst 'the adhesive viscid pulp is used as bird-lime.'
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

There are twenty-one Yūpas, all of them twenty-one cubits high; the central one (nearest to the altar) is of Rajjudāla wood: On either side are those of the Pīṭadru (tree); six of Bilva wood; three this side and three on that side - six of Khādira wood; three on this side and three on that - six, of Palāśa wood.
</details>
