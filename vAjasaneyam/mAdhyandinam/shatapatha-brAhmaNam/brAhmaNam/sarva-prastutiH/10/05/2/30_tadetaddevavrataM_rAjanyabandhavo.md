<details><summary>सर्वाष् टीकाः</summary>

<details><summary>Eggeling</summary>

10. Such, indeed, is the divine ordinance; --amongst men princes keep most aloof [^egg_727], and for that reason a vigorous (son) is born to them;

[^egg_727]: 370:1 Or, 'act most in secrecy.' Sāyaṇa explains it: manushyāṇāṁ madhye rājanyabandhavonutamāṁ gopāyanti atyarthaṁ rahasyatvena kurvanti tasmāt teshu viryavān putro jāyate. The St. Petersb. Dict., on the other hand, takes it in the sense of 'they protect most of all;' though it is difficult to see how the, 'protection' afforded by princes or rulers could have any bearing on men taking their food apart from their wives. If the above interpretation is right we may compare 'anu-gup' in the sense of 'to conceal.' See, however, the next note, where Sāyaṇa takes 'gopāyati' in the sense of 'observes (that law),' which might also have suited here. Princes, having their seraglio, would naturally have less occasion for coming into contact with their wives at mealtime than men of lower stations of life. On the superlative of the preposition, see p. 287, note 1.
</details>

<details><summary>C R Swaminathan</summary>

This is what is ordained by gods – among humans, the princely males keep themselves far away (from their mates while eating) and hence it is that vigorous son is born of them.
</details>

<details><summary>सायणः</summary>

एतद् व्रतमनुतिष्ठतां वीर्यवत्पुत्रोत्पत्तिर्भवतीत्याह - **तदेतद्देवव्रतं राजन्यबन्धव** इति । यदुक्तं 'जायाया अन्ते नाश्नीयात्' इति 'तत्' इदं देवकल्पितं व्रतम् 'एतत्' 'मनुष्याणां' मध्ये 'राजन्यबन्धवः' 'अनुतमां गोपायन्ति' अनुतमाम् अत्यर्थं रहस्यत्वेन कुर्वन्ति । 'तस्मात्' 'उ' तस्मादेव 'तेषु' 'वीर्यवान्' पुत्रो जायते 'वयसां' पक्षिणां मध्ये 'अमृतवाका'[^१_१३८] नाम पक्षिजातिरेतद्व्रतं गोपायति, अत: 'सा' 'क्षिप्रश्येनं' शीघ्रगामिनं श्येननामकं पक्षिणं 'जनयति' ॥ १० ॥

[^१_१३८]: राजहंससरस्वतीभिस्तु ‘मृतवाका' इति पदच्छेदमङ्गीकृत्य व्याख्यातम् ।
