Some notes on vināyaka kalpa in mānavagṛhya compared with vināyakaśānti in atharvan śāntikalpa:

## Origin
The texts agree with each other quite a bit and quite likely evolved from an Ur text. The procedures in vādhūlapariśiṣṭa and yājñyavalkyasmṛti apparently are also similar. So, a reconstruction of an ur text should be possible. 

Given the procedural differences between AV and YV, the yajur versions most likely cluster with each other.

AV procedure starts with recitation of a ṛk from vulgate which mānava doesn't have obviously.  

## vinAyakas
Both the texts agree that vināyakas are 4 in number. The names śālakaṭaṅkaṭa,  kūṣmāņḍarājaputra, and
Devayajana match between the two. Mānava has usmita whereas śāntikalpa has utsmṛta for one of them. 

In both, vināyakas are grabbers/seizures. 

## Symptoms
Inauspiciousness afflicts one who has been seized by vināyakas. AV performance has a recitation of symptoms. Mānava just gives them and a recitation doesn't seem to be warranted. 

The symptoms largely match. Mānava starts with "loṣṭham mṛdgāti tṛņam chinatti", which the commentator explains below (I think the symptom is more severe). 
 
The bad dreams agree with each other -seeing people with shaved/bald heads, saffron robes, matted hair etc. Seeing beasts like camel, wild ass, etc. Dreaming about climbing tall buildings (like palace) as if someone is following etc.

Princes don't get kingdom despite having qualifications. Expert teachers don't get students, students have obstacles in their studies, marriage issues, unable to have children, unfruitful harvest for farmers, loss in business ventures - these are symptoms of being under the influence of vināyakas.

## Bath
Both the texts prescribe ritual bathing of the afflicted at  crossroads. But YV version has 4 jugs of water having certain items mixed in - soil dug out by certain animals, potter's clay, gold, fragrant resin are common.

AV version has additional materials but 2 bathings. YV has "śahasrākṣam śatadhāram.." whereas AV has "pavitram śatadhāram". Interestingly at least the first part of YV appears in paippalāda samhitā. The series of mantras are given in anuṣanga and the deities largely match. 


AV has additional instructions on giving the bath from different directions. YV version doesn't allude to that. Mānava gives few mantras (in screenshot above). AV replaces that with the "lakshmi sūkta".

## Oblations
Oblations to vināyakas are made with white mustard oil at midnight. YV seems to indicate it's on the crown of the head. AV has a real homa.

Balis are offered on winnowing basket "nava śūrpa" in both - typically understood as new one but interpret it as 9 baskets. Various food, grains, meat, fish, drinks, roots like radish, drumstick etc. are offered as bali.


## Goddess
Then there's invocation of Goddess AryA in AV, with specific figurines - with 5 other Goddesses. AV version implies it's indrāņi and daughters. YV doesn't explicitly name so.  

Various food, grains, meat, fish, drinks, roots like radish, drumstick etc. are offered as bali.

But gives equivalent mantras. AV figurines are made with vetiver. Obviously the Goddesses are invoked to bring good luck. These days performers invoke the Goddess as gaurī.
The YV version also has an upasthāna of sūrya - namaste astu bhagavan shatarashme... Manava loves this
Mantra and gives it in various karmans.
The ritual is a classic atharvan one and naturally fits in the class of rituals to avert bad luck and bring good luck. It naturally deploys vulgate sūktas, though the kalpaja mantras are not found in either śaunaka or paippalāda.
This is another instance of AV and gṛhya karmas overlap we saw in previous other threads.
Also, the AV ritual IMO is quite archaic, as the good luck bringers are indrāņi and her daughters, rather than more popular Goddesses you start seeing in smārta paddhatis.
