सर्वाष् टीकाः
Gemini 3.8 Flash

With ‘Go to the pen, the cow-stall’,
the metres are indeed the pen, the cow-stall;
he makes the metres a pen, a cow-stall for him.
With ‘May thy heaven rain’,
he brings down rain itself.
With ‘Bind, O god Savitṛ, in the furthest earth with a hundred fetters’,
a man has two rivals: he whom he hates, and he who hates him;
both of these he binds for him in the furthest earth with a hundred fetters.
With ‘May not thy drop leap to heaven’,
the drop is whatever sap she has;
these creatures live upon it;
he restrains it upon her,
so that it should not leap away.
With ‘Let not Araru fly to heaven’,
Araru is indeed the rival;
he thrusts the rival away from the heavenly world.
There was an Asura named Araru;
he feared, ‘The gods will overcome us by the sacrifice.’
He smeared the earth with poison, making it impure.
Indra slew Vṛtra;
his blood ran along this [earth];
it became impure.
When he digs up,
whatever of it is impure, unsuited for sacrifice, that he smites away.
There was an Asura named Araru;
he feared, ‘The gods will thrust me out from the earth.’
He lay creeping along the earth.
Indra noticed him.
With ‘Drive away Araru, the non-sacrificial place, from the earth, the sacrificial place’, he smote him from the earth.
He flew to heaven.
With ‘Let not Araru fly to heaven’, he thrust him back from heaven.
Then the gods prospered, the Asuras were vanquished.
For whomsoever knowing thus the stambayajus is carried, and who, knowing thus, carries it, he excludes the rival from these worlds; he prospers in himself, his rival is vanquished.
This earth belonged to the Asuras;
the gods said, ‘Give us of this.’
They said, ‘Speak for yourselves.’
Agni conquered the eastern quarter;
the Vasus the southern, the Rudras the western, the Ādityas the northern.
The gods found this [earth] from the Asuras.
Then the gods prospered, the Asuras were vanquished.
He who, knowing thus, encloses the altar with these deities, wins this [earth] of his rival; he prospers in himself, his rival is vanquished.

he encloses the Āhavanīya fire.
The hips [of the altar] are westward;
he encloses the Gārhapatya fire.
The demons indeed crawl along the root;
he should not cut it with the fingernail.
If he should cut it with the fingernail, he would become one with deformed nails;
he cuts it with the wooden sword.
The wooden sword is indeed a thunderbolt;
with the thunderbolt he slays the demons.
