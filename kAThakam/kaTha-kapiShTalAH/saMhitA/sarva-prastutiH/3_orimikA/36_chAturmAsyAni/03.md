<details open><summary>विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः (यन्त्र-स्वरः)</summary>

यावत्तरसं᳓ त्वै᳓ गच्छति प्रज᳓ननमेत᳓द्धवि᳓र्य᳓द्वैश्वदेवं᳓ प्रज᳓ननमेव᳓ य᳓जते स्वां᳓ मा᳓त्रां ग᳓च्छानी᳓ति यदा᳓ सह᳓स्रं पशू᳓न्ग᳓च्छेद᳓थ वरुणप्रघासै᳓र्यजेत य᳓देव᳓ त᳓त्सह᳓स्रमँ᳓स्त᳓स्याँ᳓होऽवय᳓जत ऋतुयाजी᳓ वा᳓ अन्य᳓श्चातुर्मास्ययाज्य᳙न्यो᳓ यो᳓ वसन्तो᳓ऽभूत्प्रावृ᳓डभूच्छर᳓दभूदि᳓ति य᳓जते स᳓ ऋतुयाजी᳓ य᳓स्त्रयोदशं᳓ मा᳓सं सम्पाद᳓यति स᳓ त्रयोदशं᳓ मा᳓समभि᳓यजते स᳓ चातुर्मास्ययाजी᳓ त्री᳓नृजू᳓निष्ट्वा᳓ चतुर्थ᳓- मु᳓त्सृजेद्द्वौ᳓ प᳓रा ऋजू᳓ इष्ट्वा᳓ तृती᳓यमु᳓त्सृजेद्ये᳓ वै᳓ त्र᳓यस्संवत्सरा᳓स्ते᳓षाँ ष᳓ट्त्रिँशत्पूर्ण᳓मासा यौ᳓ द्वौ᳓ त᳓योश्च᳓तुर्विँशतिर्ये᳓ ष᳓ट्त्रिँश᳓त्य᳓धि ते च᳓तुर्विँशतिमुपय᳓न्त्येष᳓ वा᳓व᳓ स᳓ त्रयोदशो᳓ मा᳓सस्त᳓मेवै᳓त᳓त्स᳓म्पादयति त᳓मभि᳓यजते ।
</details>

<details><summary>सर्वाष् टीकाः</summary>

<details><summary>Gemini 3.8 Flash</summary>

and he attains according to his speed.  
The Vaiśvadeva is an oblation of propagation;  
he sacrifices propagation itself, [thinking:] “May I attain my full measure.”  
When he should attain a thousand cattle, then he should sacrifice with the Varuṇapraghāsas;  
what distress there is of that thousand which he attained, that he sacrifices away.  
One is a seasonal sacrificer; another is a Cāturmāsya sacrificer.  
He who sacrifices thinking: “Spring has arrived, the rainy season has arrived, autumn has arrived,” he is a seasonal sacrificer.  
He who brings about the thirteenth month, who sacrifices upon the thirteenth month, he is a Cāturmāsya sacrificer.  
Having sacrificed three regular [years], he should omit the fourth.  
Having sacrificed two further regular [years], he should omit the third.  
Those three years which there are, their full moons are thirty-six.  
Those two which there are, theirs are twenty-four.  
Those six which are over thirty, they go into the twenty-four.  
That indeed is the thirteenth month; that he brings about in this way;  
upon that he sacrifices.
</details>

<details><summary>Gemini 3.8 Flash</summary>

When he sacrifices the grass at the fore-offerings,  
he sacrifices the plants then.  
When [he sacrifices] the grass at the after-offerings,  
he sacrifices the fruit then.  
When [he sacrifices] the grass of the Vāritīs,  
whatever is born from fruit, that he sacrifices then.  
When he sacrifices the Doors,  
he sacrifices the lap then.  
When [he sacrifices] Dawn and Night,  
he sacrifices the daybreak and the sunset then.  
When [he sacrifices] the Two Pleased Ones,  
what is born and what is to be born, that he sacrifices then.  
When [he sacrifices] the Two Offerings of Refreshment,  
whatever one eats and drinks, that he sacrifices then.  
When [he sacrifices] the Two Divine Hotṛs,  
these two [worlds] indeed are the Two Divine Hotṛs;  
these two he sacrifices then.  
When [he sacrifices] the Three Goddesses,  
speech indeed is the Three Goddesses;  
speech he sacrifices then.  
The meters indeed are speech;  
the meters he sacrifices then.  
He sacrifices to Narāśaṃsa.  
Tanūnapāt, indeed, is the sacrifice extended;  
Narāśaṃsa is [the sacrifice] unextended.  
The sacrifice is extended at this time;  
therefore he sacrifices to Narāśaṃsa at the after-offerings.  
He sacrifices to Tvaṣṭṛ;  
for he transforms forms.
