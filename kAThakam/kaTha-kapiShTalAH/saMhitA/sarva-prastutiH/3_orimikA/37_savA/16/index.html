सर्वाष् टीकाः
Gemini 3.8 Flash

Those [charms] which the Asuras buried against the gods, the gods saw after.
Those which the gods [buried] against the Asuras, the Asuras did not see after.
Thence the gods flourished, the Asuras were defeated.
Where another would not see after, there he should deposit these [objects],
so that his rival does not notice.
He flourishes himself, his rival is defeated.
‘To the earth, svāhā!

सर्वाष् टीकाः
Gemini 3.8 Flash

He carries the stone all around.
For this one protects him all around. He breaks off a clod of earth of this size;
for of this size is the self.
He digs a pit of an arm’s length;
for of this size is a man.
Having put that down, then the stone, then the gold,
he deposits him in the northern half of this [earth].
It remains unburied;
he buries [it] opposite the navel.
The navel indeed holds the vital breaths, those that go upward and those that go downward,
for the retention of the vital breaths.
He touches it with gold.
Gold is immortality.
With immortality indeed he joins him together.
‘Up [with] speech! Up [with] thought!’ [he says].
These things one would bury of him; these very things he raises up.
The vedi is of the measure of the self;
for armor is of the measure of the self.
If it were not of the measure of the self, it would not be made for oneself.
As great as the vedi is, so great is the earth.
He thus placed fortresses at the ends of this [earth];
he made fortresses of the gods.
Moreover, he made these deities the guardians on every side of these directions,
for protection.
