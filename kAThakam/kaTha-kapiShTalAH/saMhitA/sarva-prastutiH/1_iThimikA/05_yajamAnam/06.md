<details><summary>सर्वाष् टीकाः</summary>

<details><summary>Gemini 3.8 Flash</summary>

The sacrifice has gone to the people of the gods;  
thence may the blessing of the sacrifice come to me!  
The sacrifice has gone to the Fathers, the serpents, the Gandharvas, the waters, the plants, the five peoples, to people;  
thence may the blessing of the sacrifice come to me!  
I grasp you for the support of the five winds;  
I grasp you for the support of the five waters;  
I grasp you for the support of the five backs;  
I grasp you for the support of the five quarters;  
I grasp you for the support of the five tribes!  
The earth is ours;  
the oblation is the gods';  
the blessings belong to the sacrificer.  
I grasp you for the support of the five-apertured caru!
</details>

<details><summary>Jamison Brereton Notes</summary>

The qualifier ‘long’ (dīrgháḥ) of wealth in d means, of course, ‘long-lasting’ (just as dīrgháṃ támaḥ [I.32.10, etc.] refers to “long(-lasting) darkness”). However, since it is implicitly contrasted here with ‘broad’ (pṛthu-budhná- ‘having a broad base’), it is clear that the image is one of physical dimensions, not merely temporal ones. And, at least for me, “long darkness” is a more striking verbal formulation than “long-lasting darkness.”
