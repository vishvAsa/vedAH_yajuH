विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः (यन्त्र-स्वरः)

सप्त᳓दशैते᳓ द्वया᳓ ग्र᳓हाः प्राजापत्या᳓स्सप्तदशो᳓ वै᳓ पु᳓रुषः प्राजापत्य᳓श्चत्ता᳓र्य᳓ङ्गानि शिरोग्रीव᳓मात्मा᳓ वा᳓क्सप्त᳓थी द᳓श प्राणा᳓ अ᳓ङ्गे᳓ ऽङ्गे वै᳓ पु᳓रुषस्य पा᳓प्मोपश्चिष्टो य᳓द्व्यतिष᳓ङ्गं ग्र᳓हान्गृह्णा᳓त्य᳓ङ्गादङ्गादेवै᳓नं पाप्म᳓नो मुञ्चति श्री᳓र्वै᳓ सो᳓मः पाप्मा᳓ सुरोपयामा᳓ य᳓दा᳓गते काले᳓ प्रा᳓ञ्चस्सो᳓मैरुद्द्र᳓वन्ति प्रत्य᳓ञ्चस्सुरोपयामैः᳓ पाप्म᳓नैवै᳓नं वि᳓पुनाति त᳓स्मादाहुर्वाजपेययाजी᳓ त्वै᳓ पूत᳓ इ᳓ति पाप्म᳓ना ह्ये᳙नं विपुना᳓ति प᳓ञ्चैते᳓ ग्र᳓हा गृह्यन्ते पा᳓ङ्क्तो यज्ञो᳓ यज्ञ᳓मेवा᳓वरुन्द्ध ऐन्द्रा᳓स्स᳓र्वे भवन्त्येकधै᳓वा᳓स्मि᳓न्निन्द्रियं᳓ दधाति ।

सर्वाष् टीकाः
Gemini 3.8 Flash

These seventeen dual cups belong to Prajāpati.
Seventeenfold, indeed, is man, belonging to Prajāpati: four limbs, head and neck, trunk, speech as the seventh, [and] ten breaths.
Upon limb after limb, indeed, evil clings to man.
In that he draws the cups alternately,
he releases him from evil from limb after limb.
Splendor, indeed, is Soma; evil is the wine cups.
In that, when the time has come, they run forward to the east with the Soma [cups], [and] backward to the west with the wine cups,
he cleanses him thoroughly from evil.
Therefore they say: ‘The Vājapeya sacrificer is purified indeed,’
for he cleanses him thoroughly from evil.
These five cups are drawn;
fivefold is the sacrifice;
he wins the sacrifice itself.
They all belong to Indra;
all at once he bestows Indra-power upon him.

सर्वाष् टीकाः
Gemini 3.8 Flash

This is the highest food of the gods, namely Soma;
this is the lowest nourishment of men, namely wine.
In that both the Soma cups and the wine cups are drawn together,
by the highest food he wins the lower nourishment.
This is the luster of priestly power, namely Soma;
wine is the impurity of food.
In that both the Soma cups and the wine cups are drawn together,
by the luster of priestly power he places luster in himself;
by the impurity of food he strikes away impurity.
He wins the world of the gods by the Soma cups, the world of men by the wine cups.
He saves himself by the Soma cups, his wife by the wine cups.
Therefore they say: ‘The Vājapeya sacrificer comes into being in yonder world,’
for by the sacrifice it has been won by him.
With the Sāvitrī verse, ‘O god Savitṛ, urge forth the sacrifice, urge forth the lord of sacrifice unto good fortune,’ he offers at each joint.
This is an omitted sacrifice;
who indeed knows how much of it they perform, how much not?
[It is] for wholeness, for impulse.
With ‘In the impulse of vigor the great mother,’ he brings down the chariot;
this is Aditi;
impelled both by her and by Savitṛ, he brings [it] down
for the winning of vigor.
With ‘Within the waters is immortality, within the waters is healing,’ he washes the horses;
the horses are born of the waters;
from their own womb he brings them forth;
and he makes them fit for offering, worthy of sacrifice.
With ‘May Vāyu [yoke] thee, or may Manu [yoke] thee,’ he yokes them;
a human is not worthy to yoke them;
with the deities he yokes them.
With ‘O Son of Waters, of swift impulse,’ he wipes their foreheads;
he repeats what has already been said before.
