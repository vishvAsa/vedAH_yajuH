<details><summary>Gemini 3.8 Flash</summary>

A nest of the sacrifice, indeed, is made when it is performed with a yajus.  
For loudly it is performed with a ṛc and with a sāman.  
When ṛcs are the puroruc verses,  
[it is] for firmness, for propping up, for not being slack.  
With a ṛc he utters forth; with a yajus he extends; he causes [it] to go by means of the cup.  
With a ṛc indeed it is drawn for the stotra; with a yajus, for the śastra.  
Therefore these two are joined,  
for the stotra and the śastra are joined.  
Turned away at this time is the sacrifice, turned away are the deities from the sacrificer at the third pressing.  
With a verse to Agni he offers around the Saumya [offering] in front, with one to Viṣṇu behind.  
Agni, indeed, is all the deities; Viṣṇu is the sacrifice.  
He grasps both the deities and the sacrifice.  
They slay Soma indeed when they press [him].  
This is his covering-cow, namely the Saumya [offering].  
He offers in the southern half of the fire,  
for the south belongs to the ancestors.  
He cuts off once,  
for what is cut off once belongs to the ancestors.  
When he cuts off a second time with the stirring-stick,  
thereby it becomes [still] cut off once.  
They carry [it] to the Udgātṛs.  
The sāman has Soma as its deity,  
[it is] for the potency of the sāman, for having its full body.  
“Should it be eaten, or should it not be eaten?”—they deliberate.  
If he should eat it, he would be prone to cough;  
if he should not eat it, it would not be an oblation.  
He should smell it;  
he does both.  
He who, being capable of [eating] food, cannot eat food, should eat it.  
That food is supreme, which the ancestors are.  
By means of the supreme food he secures the lower food.  
A remedy, indeed, did the gods make for the sacrifice.  
In that there is the Saumya [offering], that is this healer.  
Therefore it should be eaten by one who is sick.  
“May the ancestors rejoice, may those whose face is Soma rejoice!  
May we attain the life ordained by the gods!  
Drunk by Indra, discerning,  
May we attain the life ordained by the gods!

—thus he should look upon the Saumya [offering].  
What one coughs up here, what of his own self perishes, that goes to the ancestors.

On both sides he encloses [it],  
so that he does not approach.
