<details><summary>सर्वाष् टीकाः</summary>

<details><summary>Gemini 3.8 Flash</summary>

He should make half of the post-hole on the altar, half outside the altar,  
for the obtaining of the measured and the unmeasured.  
He should not make the butt visible;  
if he were to make the butt visible, it would be a pit-post.  
He should not let it go untied around [with the cord];  
if he were to let it go untied around, the sacrificer would become hungry, the adhvaryu would become naked.  
The cord is vigor;  
he ties it around against the middle;  
he places vigor in the middle, both in the sacrificer and in offspring;  
therefore vigor invigorates offspring from the middle.  
He should set it up without a cord for him whom he hates;  
the cord is vigor;  
he deprives him of vigor.
</details>

"Thou art girded around; may the divine clans gird thee around!"—[with this he ties it];  
these divine clans are the plants;  
the cord is the plants;  
the sacrificial post is the sacrificer;  
he turns offspring face to face toward the sacrificer.  
"Around this man [pour] wealth!"—  
wealth is cattle;  
he makes him prosper with cattle.  
"Glide over the back of the atmosphere!"—with this he inserts the svaru-chip;  
for it has the atmosphere as its deity at this time, being above this world, as it were, and below that one;  
with its own deity he inserts it.  
The butt belongs to the fathers;  
what is above the butt belongs to men;  
the region of the cord belongs to the plants;  
what is above the cord belongs to the All-gods;  
the head-ring belongs to Indra;  
what is leftover belongs to the Sādhyas;  
in this very manner are the fathers, men, and gods in these worlds;  
the sacrificial post belongs to all deities;  
with all deities he makes it prosper.  
He should make [the post] bent inward from the side of the fire, bent outward on the outer side;  
by what is bent inward from the side of the fire he makes room for the sacrificer; by what is bent outward on the outer side, he thrusts away the rival from the sacrifice.

<details><summary>Gemini 3.8 Flash</summary>

By means of the sacrifice the gods went to the heavenly world;  
they desired, "May no other know this world of ours!";  
they obstructed the quarters;  
because they obstructed (ayopayan) the quarters, that is why the post is called post (yūpa);  
having gone to that world, they thirsted;  
they knew, "From offerings given from there we have come here";  
they gave this [post] back;  
thereby they prospered.  
This indeed is the warp of the sacrifice;  
this is the highest footstep;  
just as a web is stretched from a peg, so from here the sacrifice is stretched.  
Formerly, pulling it down, they used to offer it [the post itself];  
in that he offers the svaru-chip,  
[it is] for the ransom of this very [post];  
ransomed for him, pleased, rendered auspicious, it attends upon these worlds.  
"May thy smoke go to the sky, thy flame to the atmosphere, thy ash to the earth, svāhā!"—  
he makes it go to these very worlds.
