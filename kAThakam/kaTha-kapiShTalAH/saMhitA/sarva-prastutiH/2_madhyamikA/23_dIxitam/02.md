<details><summary>Gemini 3.8 Flash</summary>

Wherever they intend the sacrifice, there he seizes it.  
"To insight, to mind, to Agni, svāhā!" [he says].  
With insight, with mind he approaches, [thinking,] "Let him sacrifice!"  
He seizes the sacrifice itself.  
"To consecration, to heat-austerity (tapas), to Agni, svāhā!" [he says].  
Through consecration, through heat-austerity he attains the sacrifice;  
he secures the sacrifice itself.  
"To Sarasvatī, to Pūṣan, to Agni, svāhā!" [he says].  
Speech is Sarasvatī; cattle are Pūṣan.  
Through speech the sacrifice is seized from the cattle.  
Through speech alone he seizes the sacrifice from the cattle.  
"O waters, divine, lofty, all-comforting," [he says].  
The waters are the sacrifice.  
The sacrifice is in heaven, in the intermediate space, on the earth.  
Bṛhaspati is holy power (brahman).  
Here and there is the sacrifice.  
Wherever the sacrifice is, from there he seizes it with holy power.  
He offers these five [oblations].  
Fivefold is the sacrifice.  
He secures the sacrifice itself.  
These yajuses are the requisites of the sacrifice.  
If he were not to offer them, his sacrifice would be unequipped.  
When he offers them,  
he gathers together the sacrifice as great as it is.  
One who has offered one oblation is not consecrated.  
Formerly, indeed, they used to eat the food of him for whom these are not offered.  
When he offers them,  
[it is] for consecration, for a second [state].  
"To Agni, svāhā! To Agni, svāhā!" he offers.  
Agni is all the deities.  
He secures all the deities.  
He offers these five.  
The elevation-oblation (audgrabhaṇa) is the sixth.  
Six are the seasons.  
He firmly establishes himself in the seasons.  
The sacrifice, when created, crushed the yajus, [and] crushed the sāman.  
The ṛc alone held it in check: nine sāmans, twelve yajuses.  
Therefore they chant the Bahiṣpavamāna with nine [verses].  
With an ṛc they offer the elevation-oblation.  
When he offers the elevation-oblation with an ṛc,  
he holds in check upwards with an ṛc the sacrifice that has been created.  
The Kārudveṣins used to offer the elevation-oblation with twelve [verses].  
Having taken [the ladle] filled twelve times, he should offer it with just this [verse];  
through that he attains that.  
He offers with a full ladle.  
Prajāpati is full.  
He attains Prajāpati.  
If he were to offer with an incomplete [ladle], he would make room for a rival.  
The full is attained by desire.  
Whatever desire one desires, that of the full he accomplishes.
</details>
