1. What is Aranyaka

1

Historical Back-ground:

The Vedic Literature has been divided into three parts:

(1) Samhitas or Mantras, (2) Brahmanes, (3) Aranyakas and Upanisads. Aranyakas, including the Upanisads in them, are the concluding portions of the Vedas. Samhitas contain Mantras, praises and prayers; Brahmanas contain voluminous details for sacrifices; Aranyakas meditate the mystery of the sacrifices; and the Upanisads present the highest philosophy.

A question is raised whether the Aranyakas and the

Upanisads are a protest against the Brahmanas. But this question is now out-of-date. In the beginning of the Vedic studies in Europe it was considered that the warriors, in protest of the priests, put forward the philosophical speculations; but soon it was realized that the philosophical speculations had their root, growth and development in the Ṛgveds, the Atharvavede, the other Samhites and even in the Brahmanas; and the Aranyakas and the Upanisads stand firmly on these bases; they are the result of a long thinking tradition. It is true that most philosophers are not priests, who were engaged in the long sacrificial science. But the warriors were also the masters of the sacrificial science; many proofs of this fact are recorded in the Brahmanas; for example we may see the passages of the Satapatha Brahmane, where Ayse thüņa teaches his

The seers of the Upanisads are not only kings or priests, but also women and the people of dubious descent. Though at first the Brahmanas or the priests were unable to lead the philosophical thoughts on account of being engaged in the sacrifices but soon they found their hold upon these by their wisdom. They produced the doctrine of the four Asramas (stages of life) and thus they made these philosophical speculations essential parts of the third and the fourth Asramas.

According to the orthodox view it is a revelation. whom was it revealed and who made it known to all? These are the questions which we have to discuss. In the text we find that the authorities of many seers are quoted, but the name of the compiler is unknown. According to the native tradition Vyasa was the compiler of the Vedas, but he cannot be the compiler of the Aranyakas. In this Aranyaka we find the authorities of Vyssal and even of Valaamp ay ana2 quoted. It is a proof that this Aranyaka was compiled after the se seers became famous to be quoted in a work which comes in the class of the revelation. Hence we can only say that this Aranyaka was compiled by the descendants of the Taittiriyas and the Kathas. Nothing more can be said in this respect. As regards the authorities quoted in the text we can only take them as quotations only and nothing else. so it is clear that the authorship of the Taittiriya Aranyaka is unknown. 5. Text, Commentators and Editions

The text of the Taittiriya Arany akce has been published from three places. It was first published by Rajendra Lake Mitra in Bibliotheca Indica in the year 1872 with the commentary of Say ana and with an Introduction, contexts and a short appendix.

With the commentary of Bhatta Bhaskare Misra it was publish -ed from Mysore by A. Mahadeva sastri and K. Rangacarya in 1900. These two editions are not obtainable in the market now. with the commentary of say an scarys it has been published from Anandesrams, Poons. The second edition of this appeared in 1927 and this is still in the market.

All these three editions contain some foot-notes giving the different readings, but the editors of these texts did not try to see all the Menuscripts or even the first published editions. There are misprints also in them.

Mysore edition gives the eight Prapathakes while the other two give ten Prepathakes. In the Mysore edition the three Prapathakas have been contained in one. But this is not all, there is difference also in the arrangement of the Prapathak as. The first three Prapathak as are similar to one another in all the editions; but the others are different. Bhatta Bhaskara has commented a text, which is different in arrangement than the text of Say ana’s commentary. In the Sayana’s commentary which are the Prapath akas IV and V VIII; they are in Bhatta Bhaskara’s commentary, Prapathakas VII and 1.e. the Pravargya has been contained in the Prapathak as IV and v in the text of Sayana’s commentary and in the Prapathak as VII and VIII in the text of Bhatta Bhaskara’s commentary. Bhatta Bhaskara and Sayana:-

Bhatta Bhaskara lived in the 11th century and Sayana lived in the 14th century. A question arises whether the arrangement of the Prapathakas in the commentary of Bhatta Bhaskara should be considered as reliable, for Bhatta Bhaskars lived before Sayana. But this is not easy to accept it. It may be also said that the text of the Taittiriye Aranyaka comes, perhaps in two recensions. The commentary of Bhatta Bhaskara is smaller than that of sayaṇa, but in some places that gives clear meaning.

6. Comparison with the other Aranyakas.

The Taittiriye Aranyaka has been considered as a late According to Keith the Aitareya Aranyaka is the oldest one, but other scholars consider that the Brh ad arany aka is the oldest one. C.V.Vaidya has said that the Taittiriya Aranyaka is the oldest one and it showed the way for the other sakhas to produce such if same treatises. We may say that the Taittiriys Aranyaka is/not anterior to the Brhadaranyaka then it is contemporary to that. But the Aitareya Aranyaka cannot be assigned anterior to this Aranyaka. Portions of the Chandogya Upanisad are also, perhaps, in the time in which the Brhed anyaka and the Taittiriya Arany aks are. The Sankhayana Aranyaka, Jaiminiyopanisad-Brahmena (or the Talavikara Aranyaka), the Maitreyaniya Aranyaka (or the Brh ad aranyska of the Caraka sakha) and the Katha Arenyaks are posterior to the Taittiriya Aranyaka.
