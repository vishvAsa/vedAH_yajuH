The contents of the Prapathak as in brief:-

The first Prapathaka contains the Mantras and the Brahman as of the Arunake tuka fire-piling.

The second Prapathake deals with the Upavite, Kusmanda Homa (Prayascittas), Vedic study, Brahma sacrifice, interruption of study and five gre et sacrifices'.

In the third Prapathaka there are the Mantras of the

Caturhotra fire-piling, also with the Mantras for the human sacrifice, for the animal sacrifice for the Brahmamedha.

The fourth and the fifth Prapathak as contain respectively the Mantras and the Brahman as of the Pravargya sacrifice. In the sixth Prapathaka there are the Mantras of the

Pitrmedha.

The next four Prapathak as deal with the mystic knowledge

(Upanisad).

Arunaketuka fire and the Up an ay ana are met with in this

Aranyaka at first. Kusmanda Home and five great sacrifices are also of great interest, for in the later time they took main part in the life of the Hindus. The Caturhotra, the Pavargya and the Pitrmedha are described in other vedic treatises also. Besides these there are many little Homes for obtaining intellect, brilliance, cattle, prosperity, wealth etc. The Brahma-medha of the Prapathaka II and the self-sacrifice of the Prapathaka X are enough to prove that the tendency was to obtain knowledge instead of going through the great details of the sacrifices. In the chapter of the critical study I have described these sacrifices and the translation of the text I have given in footnotes the necessary informations regarding the use of the Mantras in particular sacrifices; hence I do not want to repeat them here.

The subjects discussed:-

Many interesting subjects from the spheres of the life occur scattered hither and thither and also in regular descriptions at some places. They come only in auxilliary forms of the sacrificial dealings except those philosophical matters of the Upanisads. Observing these subjects and descriptions we find many traces which have been developed afterwards in the Puranas and the epics. Many gods, mythologies and the Avatares (incarnations) have their origin in this Aranyaka as well as in other Vedic literatures. These subjects have been arranged in proper classes and have been dealt with in the chapter of the critical study. They have been also shown in the 'content'; hence I need not repeat them here.
