## Books of related interest

TAITTIRĪYA ĀRAṆYAKA

With the Comm. of Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara Miśra and an Eng. Introduction by *T.N. Dharmadhikari*

(3 volumes bound in one)

Edited by *A. Mahadeva Sastri* and *K. Rangacarya*

THIS TREATISE belongs to the Taittirīya School of the Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda and is 'āraṇyaka' because it is to be recited by a Vrati in the midst of araṇya (forest). This is a reprint of the earlier edition brought out in the Government Oriental Library Series Nos. 26, 27 and 29, Mysore. In this edition, the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka has been divided into 8 Prapāṭhakas. Prapāṭhaka 1 deals with Aruṇaketucayana. Prapāṭhaka 2, i.e. Svādhyāya Brāhmaṇa deals with the rite of Sacred Thread, Twilight Worship, Kuṣmāṇḍa Sacrifice, Initiation and vows. These two Prapāṭhakas belong to the Kaṭha School of Black Yajurveda. Prapāṭhaka 3 compiles formulas for Caturhotraciti, Puruṣakṛticiti, Upahoma. Caturhotraciti is the same as Brahmaciti or Brahmamedha. Prapāṭhaka 4 deals with the Pitṛmedha and cremation rites. Prapāṭhaka 5 comprises the Taittirīya Upaniṣad, divided into 3 vallis: Śikṣā, Brahma and Bhṛgu. Prapāṭhaka 6 constitutes, Nārāyaṇopaniṣad known as Yājñiki. Prapāṭhakas 7, 8 contain the Pravargya formulas and their Brāhmaṇa.

TAITTIRĪYA BRĀHMAṆA

(Text in Devanāgari: by *A Mahadeva Sastri; R. Shama Sastri and L. Srinivasacharya*) With the commentary of *Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara Miśra*

THE BRĀHMAṆAS, as is well-known, are a class of ritualistic literature containing priestly discussions regarding the details of sacrifices, myths and legends associated with them, rules for their performance as well as symbolic explanations and speculative reasons in their support. To each of the four Vedic Saṃhitās belong many Brāhmaṇas originating from various schools (Śākhās). The *Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa* belongs to the Black Yajurveda. It is a continuation of the Taittirīya Saṃhitā and is, therefore, aptly named as "anubrāhmaṇa" by the tradition. It contains only later additions to the Saṃhitā including the symbolic "Human Sacrifice" *(Puruṣamedha)*. The extant Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa is divided into three Kāṇḍas (or Aṣṭakas) which are further divided into 28 Prapāṭhakas and 338 Anuvākas.

## Preface

[[Pix]]

The Taittirīya Āraṇyaka contains several elements from a later origin than the Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa and Saṃhitā. The recognition of this by scholars like A. Weber and A. B. Keith, may have been the reason for their relative neglect of this part of the Taittirīya tradition. However, the mantras and Brāhmaṇa of the Pravargya ritual have found their place in the Āraṇyaka probably not because they originated later, but because of the special character of the Pravargya ritual. The Pravargya is one of the few rituals that has been explicitly referred to in the Ṛgveda (usually under the name "Gharma"). The abstract and spiritual nature of what is probably the main purpose of the ritual the participants should acquire the lustre of the sun the simple means by which it can be performed and the way the main implement, the pot which is heated red-hot, is worshipped, give it a special place in the vast assortment of Vedic rites.

For a complete description of the Pravargya according to the Śrauta Sūtras, the reader is referred to van Buitenen's *The Pravargya* (Poona, 1968) and the *Śrautakośa*. The present book gives a translation and discussion of the Pravargya Brāhmaṇa of the Taittirīya tradition. It is, depending on the recension, either the fifth or the eighth chapter of the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka. The text discusses the use of the Pravargya mantras and abounds in Brāhmaṇical explanations of the mantras and acts. It follows the ritual in an older, slightly different version than the Śrauta Sūtras. The translation and introduction intend to make the text accessible to students of comparative cultural history, to Sanskrit students who are not specialists in the field of Vedic ritual (the Sanskrit text has been given with the translation), and to others who might be interested in the subject. Scholarly problems connected with the text and its subject are discussed in notes at the end.
