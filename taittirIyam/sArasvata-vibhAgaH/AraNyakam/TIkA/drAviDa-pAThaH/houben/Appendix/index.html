1: The Place of Sacrifice

The Place of Sacrifice for a Pravargya that forms part of a Soma Sacrifice The regular performances of the Pravargya (TĀ 5, anuvāka 4-8) take all place in the Pracinavamsa, except that the Adhvaryu and his assistant leave the hut through one of the doors, in order to milk the cow and goat (see anuvāka 7, and note 81). The twice daily heating of the Pravargya vessel takes place on the western or pravṛijaniya mound. At the end of each performance, except at the time of the final disposal, the implements are put on the Samraḍ-asandi or Emperor’s Throne. The Patni-sālā (Wife’s Hall) is at the west of the Prācina-vamsa.

The previously prepared clay-pit, from which the clay is procured for the preparation of the implements, is at a certain distance east of the Āhavanīya. To the north of this place is the black antelope skin, spread out with its neck towards the east and the hairy side upwards (anuvāka 2). At the time of the preparation of the implements, the Mahāvedi (Great Altar Space), the Uttaravedi, the Sadas (Hall of Recitation) and Havirdhāna (Soma Hall) are not yet meted out and put up. The ingredients are mixed and the implements are formed out of the clay-mixture on a mound to the north or north-east of the place of sacrifice. The implements are baked in a fire-pit, dug to the east of the Gārhapatya fire (BSS: to the north of the hut).

The disposal of the implements after the last performance (anuvāka 9 and 10) normally takes place at the by now constructed Uttaravedi. The Sadas and the Havirdhana are put up afterwards.

The plan shows the Pracinavamsa where the main actions of the Pravargya take place, and some places that will play a role in the Soma sacrifice to which the Pravargya is the introductory part.

The Samraḍ-asandi, the Emperor’s Throne for the Pravargya vessels, is according to some not east but west (HirŚS) or north (KātyāyanaŚS; optional in VaikhŚS) of the King’s Throne or Rājāsandi.

The peg for the cow is south of the middle of the southern door, according to the Sūtras within the sight of the Hoty. The pin for the calf is

2: Concordance of the three editions

The Pravargya Mantras and Brāhmaṇa of the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka Concordance of the three editions C is the Calcutta edition, M the Mysore edition, P the Poona édition (see Abbreviations under TĀ). Prap. is the prapāṭhaka, An. the anuvāka, Da. the daśati; in the case of the Pravargya mantras, the last number (between parentheses), is the number a specific part of the text has in edition C or M. The letters a, b, c… are attributed by me to the pādas or portions between two dandas, if they have to be indicated specifically. “om” means that a certain part has been omitted or is absent. In case a number appears - by mistake - only in the notes and not in the text, the number has been marked with *. C and P (both with S’s commentary) are almost similar, except that P does not indicate the numbers of the different portions of the text. Other differences (which are very few) have been indicated between parentheses.
