## Anuvāka 1: The Pravargya as the head of the Sacrifice

This anuvāka contains the mythological story of the origin and purpose, the *purākalpa*, of the Pravargya ceremony in the form of the story of Makha. See Introduction section 3.4 and Hillebrandt, *Vedische Mythologie*:414-54; Keith, Religion:112; Rönnow, “Zur Erklärung des Pravargya etc.":113ff, esp. 127; Eggeling, *SBE* 44, p. xlvi-l; van Buitenen, *Pravargya*:16ff; Gonda, Aspects: 167ff; Kashikar, “Mahāvīra"; and Heesterman, "The Case of the Severed Head" in: Essays in *Indian* Ritual, *Kingship and* Society, Chicago 1985. In the introduction of the TĀ edition C (p. 27-28), a translation of this anuvāka is quoted from Muir, *Sanskrit* Texts IV, p. 112.

If one places the Pravargya vessel on the fire, then one puts back the head of the sacrifice. Offering that sacrifice united with its head, one gains blessings, one conquers the celestial world.

Therefore (because they put the head of the sacrifice on the body), this Pravargya is strong (in its mantras and sacrificial acts) for the Aśvins, as it were.
